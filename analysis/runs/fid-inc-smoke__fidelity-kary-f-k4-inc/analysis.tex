\begin{abstract}
This is the smallest run in my set --- 43 events, 0.032\,s, three operator applications ---
and it is a smoke test of the \texttt{--incompressible} payload generator and the new
value-level retention metrics ahead of the broker campaign that ran ten minutes later. The
executor is \texttt{function}~$\times$~8, so it measures the protocol and the $k$-ary
algorithm's arithmetic, not agent behaviour. On that arithmetic it is exactly right:
\texttt{fold\_depth: 2} $= \lceil\log_4 8\rceil$ against the binomial sibling's 3 and the
chain's 7, three applications $= \lceil (p-1)/(k-1)\rceil = \lceil 7/3\rceil$ recorded as
two \texttt{merge:k4:d1} calls plus one \texttt{merge:k2:d2}, seven messages $= p-1$, and
\texttt{variadic: true} confirming the four-way kernel actually ran rather than degrading to
a left fold. Retention is 1.000 with \texttt{n\_intact: 96} and \texttt{n\_dropped: 0}, and
that is a plumbing result rather than a fidelity one: the surrogate kernel never sees the
token budget, so its outputs are 780, 780 and 2{,}043 tokens against a nominal ceiling of
450, and the recovered accumulator for the $4\to0$ message carries all five candidate items
--- four whole input reports plus the prompt's own instruction line --- so nothing was
discarded at all. The run does, however, expose a real defect in the cost model, and it is
the one my other $k$-ary documents point back to: the collectives table reports 2 rounds
against a prediction of 1, because \texttt{cost.py} evaluates the tabulated \texttt{("reduce",
"kary")} entry at \texttt{DEFAULT\_FANIN = 8} rather than at the fan-in the run used, and the
run-level finding still announces that every checkable collective agrees with its cost
expression, because \texttt{Analysis.model\_checks} aggregates only \texttt{messages\_agree}.

\section{Interpretation}

The counts are by construction and worth checking explicitly, because \texttt{kary} appears
in no collective sweep and these fidelity cells are its only sweep-adjacent evidence. Fold
depth: the \texttt{kary} branch advances \texttt{stride} by factors of $k$ and increments
depth once per level, giving $\lceil\log_4 8\rceil = 2$, and the \texttt{coll.reduce} payload
records 2. Applications: each variadic call consumes $k$ inputs and retires $k-1$, so a
$p$-rank fold needs $\lceil (p-1)/(k-1)\rceil = 3$ at $p=8$ with $k=4$, and the trace holds
exactly three. The labels carry the actual arity, so the degenerate two-way top level ---
$p$ is not a power of $k$ here --- is visible as \texttt{merge:k2:d2} rather than having to
be inferred; a clean $k=4$ measurement needs $p=16$, which is what
\texttt{fid-synth\_\_fidelity-kary-f-k4} provides. Messages: seven, matching $p-1$.

Retention 1.000 is not a fidelity result, for the same two reasons that hold across this
smoke test. The budget is not binding: at commit \texttt{90ca469} the harness passed the
module-level \texttt{MERGE\_CONTRACT}, which has no \texttt{max\_tokens} field, so the 450
tokens existed only as a sentence in the prompt and a \texttt{meta} hint --- the advisory
regime --- which is why a 2{,}043-token output records \texttt{attempt: 1} and the run
reports zero contract violations. And the operator is not budget-aware:
\texttt{\_surrogate\_merge} takes a drop rate and a seed and grows without limit rather than
choosing what to shed. Whole-rank erasure in this experiment is what happens when an
accumulator standing for many ranks fills a binding budget, so with neither condition met the
mechanism cannot fire, and \texttt{--incompressible} --- whose purpose is to remove the
operator's ability to re-encode its way out of pressure --- is inert. The recovered
accumulator for the $4\to0$ message makes this concrete: five findings, 49 identifiers, ranks
4 through 7 complete plus the phantom \texttt{[F-3-2]} copied out of the prompt's own
instruction line, which is to say the kernel discarded nothing in its first application and
its output is simply its four inputs quoted inside one another. The comparison this clears
the way for is \texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc\_\_fidelity-kary-f-k4-inc}: same algorithm, same
$p$, same twelve incompressible items per rank, same nominal 450 tokens, but with
\texttt{max\_tokens: 450} in the contract and real agents behind it, and there the three
applications return 449, 437 and 437 tokens, retention is 0.3854, and ranks 4 through 7 are
erased whole.

The defect this run exposes is in the cost model rather than the algorithm, and it matters
because it is the only place the $k$-ary implementation is checked against a closed form. The
collectives table shows rounds 2 against a prediction of 1 with \texttt{rounds\_agree: false}
in \texttt{metrics.json}, and yet the model column reads \modelok\ and the run-level finding
states that all checkable collectives sent exactly the number of messages their cost
expression predicts. Both halves are wrong in different ways. The prediction is for a
different experiment: the tabulated \texttt{("reduce", "kary")} entry in \texttt{cost.py}
evaluates $\lceil\log_k p\rceil$ at \texttt{DEFAULT\_FANIN = 8}, not at the fan-in the run
used, so it predicts the single-application depth-1 tree while the run performed the $k=4$
two-level one. \texttt{predict\_kary(p, n, k)} takes an explicit $k$ and exists precisely for
this, but \texttt{analysis.py} consults \texttt{FORMULAS}. And the reassurance is
uninformative: \texttt{Analysis.model\_checks} counts only \texttt{messages\_agree}, so a
round-count disagreement is recorded in the table and in \texttt{metrics.json} and never
reaches the findings list. The same mismatch is present in the \texttt{sat-mb} and
\texttt{inc-mb} $k$-ary cells, and is hidden by coincidence in the $p=16$ one, where
$\lceil\log_8 16\rceil$ and $\lceil\log_4 16\rceil$ are both 2.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is a surrogate, so nothing here is agent behaviour, and the \$0.0637 is a
modelled price for tokens produced by concatenation. Three applications on one trial is also
the smallest sample in my set; no distributional claim should be made from it.

The kernel's loss model, and hence the meaning of retention 1.000, rests on reading the
source and on the message payloads rather than on logged calls, because a \texttt{function}
executor writes no rows to \texttt{agent\_calls}: this run's fabric contains no prompts and
no results. What is directly observed is that the $4\to0$ payload has five findings and 49
identifiers with the instruction sentence present, and that the two depth-1 applications had
identical prompt sizes (773 tokens) and identical output sizes (780). That equality is worth
one caution: the kernel seeds its generator from \texttt{seed + len(prompt)}, so structurally
identical applications make identical draws --- in the binomial sibling four prompts of
1{,}695 characters all dropped the same candidate, and in the $p=16$ $k$-ary cell two
identical prompts erased ranks 0 and 4 together. Here the identical draw happened to keep
everything, so the defect is invisible in the result even though the mechanism is present.

Finally, this run's \texttt{value\_retention: 1.0} and \texttt{n\_label\_only: 0} are the
first exercise of the value-level metric block, and a smoke test returning the trivial answer
does not show it can return a non-trivial one. That demonstration is
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc}, where \texttt{n\_dropped: 59} with \texttt{n\_label\_only: 0}
establishes that the enforced-budget failure is clean deletion rather than a label surviving
without its payload --- the distinction the metric was added to draw.
