\begin{abstract}
This is the manager-summarises-everything cell of the saturation attempt, and because its
root folds in one rank at a time it is the run that measures the campaign's central
mechanism directly. Eight ranks contribute twelve items each; rank 0 performs all seven
merges in sequence; retention is 1.000 with no fabricated identifiers. Recovering the seven
accumulators from the fabric gives item counts of 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84 and 96 --- every
rank fully absorbed --- against output sizes of 354, 425, 373, 454, 535, 617 and 698
tokens. Two things follow. The operator re-encodes as pressure rises, from 39.3 bytes per
item at the first merge to 14.3 at the last, converging on a packed table
(\texttt{[F-0-0]100n [F-0-1]101d \dots}) whose marginal cost is a flat 81 tokens per
additional rank, or 6.8 tokens per item. And having re-encoded as far as it can, it crosses
its 450-token budget at the fourth merge and overruns monotonically to 698 tokens, 55\%
over, without a single rejection --- because the contract stored in this run's fabric
records \texttt{max\_tokens: null} and an empty semantics string, so the budget existed only
as a sentence in the prompt. That makes this run half of the most informative pair in my
set. Its twin \texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc\_\_fidelity-flat-f-k4-inc} is the same algorithm
at the same $p$ with the same nominal budget, but enforced and with incompressible content:
it saturates at the same point in the fold, cannot overrun, and instead returns the
identical accumulator four times while ranks 4 through 7 are silently erased. One knob ---
whether the runtime checks the number --- separates retention 1.000 from retention 0.385.
Note also that \texttt{flat} is not a one-shot $p$-way fold: \texttt{algorithms.py} applies
the binary operator $p-1$ times at the root, so this cell has \texttt{fold\_depth: 7} with
\texttt{rounds: 1}.

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows one occupied lane and seven empty ones. Rank 0 carries a
train of seven contiguous bars across the whole 197.62\,s; ranks 1 to 7 carry two or three
instantaneous ticks each inside the first 40\,ms and nothing after. That is the flat
reduction drawn literally: every non-root rank sends its 387-token report to the root and
returns, their \texttt{coll.reduce} records \texttt{fold\_depth: 0}, and the root does all
the work. Peak ranks busy is 1 of 8, serial fraction of active time 100\%, load imbalance
8.00$\times$ --- the worst attainable at $p=8$ and correctly reported --- and rank 0's
161.56\,s of busy time is the only non-zero entry in the per-rank table. The related
\texttt{coordination\_share} of 12.5\% understates the waste for a different reason: the
seven non-root ranks return from the reduce in milliseconds, so their 197\,s of idleness
never enters \texttt{collective\_rank\_seconds}, where the chain's blocked receives push
the same quantity to 61.9\%. This cell is far more wasteful of the pool than the chain and
that particular metric says the reverse.

The bars are nearly equal in width while the work behind them is not, and that is worth
stating because it contradicts the natural expectation. The seven merges took 27.79, 24.61,
24.51, 20.46, 20.42, 21.23 and 22.53\,s on prompts growing from 933 to 1{,}059 tokens and
outputs growing from 354 to 698. There is no upward drift: the longest call is the first.
The calibration block explains it, fitting a fixed cost of about 21\,s against a
token-dependent term that only begins to matter above 2{,}788 tokens, an order of magnitude
above anything here. The prompt stays near 900 tokens throughout even as the accumulator
absorbs eight ranks, because the accumulator itself is being compressed as fast as it
grows. Of the 197.6\,s, 161.6\,s is generation and 34.3\,s is waiting for a worker to claim
a queued call, which I obtained by differencing every
\texttt{broker.enqueue}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.claim} pair in the log; so unlike the chain
sibling this run's wall time is mostly real model time. The 27 reattachments and the six
events after \texttt{job.finish} at 197.62\,s --- the log runs to 305\,s --- are the pool
recycling, and they are why the dashboard shows all eight ranks red and suspected
\texttt{fail\_stop} while \texttt{metrics.json} records \texttt{ok: true} and no failures.

The communication matrix, Figure~\ref{fig:comm}, is a single column: seven edges into rank
0 at exactly 387 tokens each, 2{,}709 in total, and no accumulator ever crosses the fabric.
That is the least traffic of the four cells --- against the tree's 3{,}004, the $k$-ary
tree's 2{,}913 and the chain's 3{,}645 --- and it is the trade the cost model predicts: one
communication round bought by putting all seven applications on one rank's critical path.

\section{Interpretation}

The structure is by construction and both checks pass: seven messages against $p-1=7$ and
one round against 1, so \texttt{model\_checks\_agree} is 1 of 1. The construction most in
need of naming is the one the hypothesis usually gets wrong about this algorithm. A flat
reduce is often described as folding all $p$ inputs at the root in a single step, which
would make it the shallowest arrangement available and the natural upper bound on
retention. The implementation does no such thing: the \texttt{flat} branch of
\texttt{reduce} gathers all $p$ contributions, sorts them by source rank because the
operator is declared non-commutative, and then applies the \emph{binary} kernel $p-1$ times
in sequence, setting \texttt{fold\_depth = p - 1 = 7} and \texttt{rounds = 1}. The trace
confirms it directly --- seven \texttt{agent.call} events on rank 0 labelled
\texttt{merge:d1} through \texttt{merge:d7} --- so this is a depth-7 cell sharing its depth
with the chain, and the campaign contains three distinct depths (2, 3, 7) rather than four.

What the campaign varied, and whether it saturated, has to come out of the config and the
store. Against \texttt{real-mb-fidelity} --- same $p$, same executor, same algorithms ---
this campaign raised \texttt{facts} from 4 to 12 and lowered \texttt{merge\_budget} from 900
to 450, and added \texttt{kary} at \texttt{fanin: 4}. The root must render 96 items into
half the space the baseline had for 32, and the baseline's accumulators cost about 19.4
tokens per item, which puts the 450-token capacity near 23 items. It should have saturated
four times over. It did not, and this cell shows both reasons in a single monotone
sequence. Bytes per identifier fall 39.3, 29.0, 16.4, 15.6, 15.0, 14.6, 14.3 across the
seven merges: the operator abandons prose for a packed table and, once it has, the marginal
cost of admitting another rank settles at exactly 81 tokens --- 454, 535, 617, 698 --- or
6.8 tokens per item. Extrapolating that line backwards gives roughly $49 + 6.8n$ tokens for
$n$ items, so the 450-token budget is first crossed at about 59 items, and indeed the
fourth merge is the first to exceed it at 454 tokens for 60. The remaining three overrun by
19\%, 37\% and 55\%. Every one of them records \texttt{attempt: 1}. The contract in the
fabric reads \texttt{\{"name": "MergedReport", \dots, "max\_tokens": null, \dots,
"semantics": ""\}} against \texttt{meta} of \texttt{\{"max\_tokens": 450\}}: the budget
reached the executor as a generation hint a worker may ignore, and never reached the
checker that would have rejected it. Under a null bound, ``zero contract violations'' is
uninformative rather than reassuring, and \texttt{make\_tables.py} is right to classify
this block empirically as \emph{not capacity-bound}.

The comparison that makes this cell valuable is with its own twin in the corrected
campaign. \texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc\_\_fidelity-flat-f-k4-inc} is the same algorithm, the
same $p=8$, the same twelve items per rank and the same nominal 450 tokens, differing in
exactly two respects: the contract carries \texttt{max\_tokens: 450} with a non-empty
semantics clause, and the payload is high-entropy serials that cannot be paraphrased
shorter. Its fold saturates at the same place --- 437 tokens after absorbing rank 3, where
this one reaches 454 after absorbing rank 4 --- and then the two diverge completely. This
run overruns and keeps everything; that one cannot overrun, has nothing left to compress,
and so returns the byte-identical accumulator for \texttt{merge:d4} through
\texttt{merge:d7}, erasing ranks 4 to 7 whole while every call still satisfies its contract.
Retention 1.000 against 0.385, from one knob. That is the sharpest available demonstration
that what governs retention is the enforced output budget rather than the shape of the fold
tree, and it is why the depth-7 flat fold, the depth-3 tree and the depth-2 $k$-ary tree
score identically within each campaign.

On cost this cell is the campaign's second-worst and has no compensating advantage. Seven
applications, all serial at the root: 197.62\,s against the binomial tree's 77.56\,s and the
$k$-ary tree's 53.07\,s, at \$0.0709 against \$0.0625 and \$0.0369, on 6{,}353 prompt tokens.
It is faster than the chain's 1{,}376.98\,s, but that comparison is worthless because
1{,}100\,s of the chain is a worker failing to appear. Of the flagged findings, the
serial-execution warning is the algorithm's definition rather than a defect and is worth
keeping for a reader meeting this pattern in a real harness; the 8.00$\times$ imbalance is
honest; the 27 reattachments are the broker's normal mode; and the model check is the check
passing.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is real (\texttt{broker}~$\times$~8, \texttt{worker}~$\times$~27), so this is
agent behaviour rather than a surrogate implementing a loss model, as the
\texttt{fid-synth} and \texttt{fid-inc-smoke} cells do. The most serious threat is that
retention 1.000 does not measure information transport. \texttt{make\_fact\_report} builds
item $i$ of rank $r$ with throughput $100 + 7r + i$ under workload
\texttt{['nominal','degraded','saturated'][i \% 3]}, so the payload is a deterministic
function of the identifier, and the packed encoding reproduces the rule literally ---
\texttt{[F-0-0]100n [F-0-1]101d \dots [F-0-11]111s}, then \texttt{[F-1-0]107n \dots}. This
cell is the one where that matters most, because its accumulator is a strictly increasing
rank prefix in a visibly arithmetic sequence: after four ranks the root has seen 100--111,
107--118, 114--125 and 121--132, and the next rank's twelve values are trivially
predictable. A root that had lost rank 6's message entirely could emit \texttt{F-6-0}
through \texttt{F-6-11} with correct values and score as having retained them. Every
retention claim here is a claim about identifier survival on a guessable payload; the
\texttt{-inc} variant exists precisely because of this, and there the payload is scored as
well as the label.

Second, two of this run's seven results are byte-identical to results in sibling cells:
\texttt{725368554341} (373 tokens, ranks 0--3) is also the binomial cell's
\texttt{merge:d2} and the $k$-ary cell's \texttt{merge:k4:d1}, and \texttt{e411144037d6}
(698 tokens, all 96 items) is also the binomial cell's \texttt{merge:d3} and the $k$-ary
cell's \texttt{merge:k2:d2}. The four cells ran sequentially against one persistent pool of
Cursor subagents, so reuse was mechanically possible, and here three structurally different
prompts --- a fold of an accumulator with a leaf, a fold of two subtree accumulators, and a
four-way variadic fold --- produced the same object. I ran the determinism test the contract
requires and it comes out against determinism: prompt digest \texttt{edf28c1217be} is shared
between this run's \texttt{merge:d1} and the binomial cell's, and the results differ sharply
(354 tokens against 211, different digests). Identical input does not give identical output
here, so determinism cannot explain the identities. Convergence on the canonical packed
encoding can, and is consistent with the identities occurring only among the dense late
accumulators; cross-run reuse cannot be excluded from the trace.

Third, the 81-tokens-per-rank marginal cost is fitted to four points, from 60 to 96 items,
after the operator has settled into the packed encoding; the first three merges do not lie
on that line and the third is 52 tokens \emph{below} the second despite carrying twelve
more items. The sibling cell's four structurally identical depth-1 merges returned 211, 269,
356 and 491 tokens on the same prompt structure, a 2.3$\times$ spread, so per-item cost in
the compressible regime is a choice the operator makes afresh and not a constant. The
crossing point of 59 items is therefore accurate for this run and should not be read as a
capacity for the configuration.

Finally, the usual accounting caveats. Every \texttt{msg.recv} records \texttt{admitted:
false} because the collective's internal receives pass \texttt{admit=False}, so context
occupancy reads 0\% even on a root that consumed 6{,}353 prompt tokens; this run says
nothing about admission pressure, which is a real gap given that a flat reduction is
exactly the pattern whose root should exhaust its context first. The \$0.0709 is modelled
from \$3/Mtok in and \$15/Mtok out with \texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}, not billed. And
\texttt{rank\_position\_correlation: 0.000} is not a fairness measurement but an undefined
quantity, since at retention 1.000 the per-rank variance is zero and the code returns 0.0
by a guard.
