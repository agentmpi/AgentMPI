\begin{abstract}
This is the depth-3 cell of the only configuration in the whole reduction-fidelity
experiment that actually loses information: eight ranks, twelve high-entropy items each,
a 450-token merge budget \emph{enforced} by contract rather than suggested in a prompt.
Retention is 0.385, and the aggregate is the least interesting thing about it. Ranks 0, 1
and 2 are retained completely, rank 3 keeps one item of twelve, and ranks 4 through 7
contribute nothing whatever. I recovered every intermediate from the fabric, and the
mechanism is visible in a single blob digest: the root's final merge, which was handed the
37-item accumulator for ranks 0--3 and the 38-item accumulator for ranks 4--7, returned
result \texttt{9f300f3a45bf} --- byte-for-byte its own left input, 437 tokens, 37
identifiers. Half the population was discarded in one application that satisfied its
contract and reported success. The reason is arithmetic rather than algorithmic: an
incompressible item costs 11.81 tokens in situ, so a 450-token ceiling admits 37 or 38 of
them and no more, and that ceiling binds at whichever application is last regardless of
how many preceded it. This cell's three fold levels and the chain's seven and the $k$-ary
tree's two all land on the same 37 survivors, so the tree-shape hypothesis is not merely
unsupported here, it is refuted by construction of the budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is the textbook binomial tree drawn in wall clock, and it is the
only cell in this campaign with real concurrency. Three bands of work: ranks 0, 2, 4 and 6
all busy together from about $t = 1$\,s to $t = 26$\,s, then ranks 0 and 4 together to
about $t = 55$\,s, then rank 0 alone to 80.27\,s. Peak ranks busy is 4 of 8, achieved
parallelism 1.90$\times$, and the serial fraction of active time is 45.3\% --- the
three-level structure means the last level is necessarily serial. Ranks 1, 3, 5 and 7 show
one tick each within the first 20\,ms and then nothing: they are the leaves of the tree,
they send their twelve-item report to their parent and return, and their
\texttt{coll.reduce} records \texttt{fold\_depth: 0}. That is why load imbalance is
2.94$\times$ and 76.2\% of the rank-seconds paid for went unused; both are what a binomial
tree at $p=8$ must produce and neither is a defect.

The bars do not grow the way a naive reading would predict, and that is the run's first
substantive detail. The four depth-1 merges took 16.22, 19.57, 23.36 and 25.24\,s on
prompts of exactly 446 tokens each; the two depth-2 merges took 19.45 and 28.39\,s on 708
and 714 tokens; the final depth-3 merge took 20.49\,s on 1{,}014 tokens. Latency is flat
in prompt size across a 2.3$\times$ range, because the output is pinned near the contract
ceiling at every level --- 288--294 tokens at depth 1, 437 and 449 at depths 2 and 3 ---
and generation, not prompt ingestion, dominates. The whole run cost 152.7\,s of
generation and only 13.7\,s of waiting for a worker to claim a queued call (I decomposed
each \texttt{broker.enqueue}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.claim}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.complete}
triple from the log), so unlike its chain sibling this cell's wall time is honest model
time. The 18 reattachments and the log's three events after \texttt{job.finish} at
80.27\,s are the worker pool churning after the job is over; the dashboard's rank health
table shows all eight ranks red and suspected \texttt{fail\_stop} for that reason, while
\texttt{metrics.json} records \texttt{ok: true} and no failures.

The communication matrix, Figure~\ref{fig:comm}, is the tree's adjacency: $1\!\to\!0$,
$3\!\to\!2$, $5\!\to\!4$, $7\!\to\!6$ at 178 tokens each, then $2\!\to\!0$ at 312 and
$6\!\to\!4$ at 313, then $4\!\to\!0$ at 469, for 1{,}806 tokens over seven messages. The
edge weights are the story in miniature: a leaf carries 12 items in 178 tokens, a
half-subtree carries 24 in 312, and the quarter-population edge $4\!\to\!0$ carries 38 in
469. Traffic grows sublinearly in the number of items represented because the accumulator
is capped, and the last edge is already at the cap.

\section{Interpretation}

Everything structural here is by construction and both checkable predictions hold: seven
messages against a closed form of $p-1=7$, three rounds against $\lceil\log_2 8\rceil=3$,
so \texttt{model\_checks\_agree} is 1 of 1 and Figure~\ref{fig:collectives} carries no red
diamond. What must be established before anything else is which enforcement regime this
run is in, because the entire interpretation turns on it, and the fabric settles it. Every
one of the seven \texttt{agent\_calls} rows records \texttt{contract} =
\texttt{\{"name": "MergedReport", \dots, "max\_tokens": 450, \dots, "semantics": "findings
must contain complete, verbatim items\dots"\}}. That is the enforced form: the runtime
checks the returned token count, rejects an overrun, and re-issues the call with the
diagnosis appended. Contrast the \texttt{sat-mb-fidelity} siblings, which ran the same
$p=8$, twelve-items, 450-token configuration with \texttt{max\_tokens: null} and an empty
semantics string, and scored 1.000 by overrunning the stated budget by up to 55\%. The
provenance block records commit \texttt{f121647}, in which \texttt{merge\_contract} does
not yet exist; the enforced contract was committed two minutes after this campaign wrote
its results, as \texttt{561d8a17}, ``A budget stated in a prompt is not a budget''. The
commit field is HEAD at launch, not the working tree, and the fabric is the authority.

What emerged is that retention under an enforced budget is a division, not a property of
the tree. An incompressible item is \texttt{[F-0-0] serial 597623 checksum LVPG}: nine
tokens of content with no shorter equivalent, 11.81 tokens once quoted inside the JSON
array. A 450-token ceiling therefore admits $450/11.81 = 38.1$ items, and every merge in
this run lands exactly there --- 437 tokens for 37 items and 449 for 38, at depths 2 and
3. The reduction never fails to fill its budget and never exceeds it; it simply stops. So
the surviving count is fixed at 37 and the aggregate is $37/96 = 0.385$ before the
algorithm is chosen. Reconstructing the fold from the store makes the consequence concrete.
The two depth-1 merges under rank 0 return 24 identifiers each; their combination at depth
2 returns 37, so eleven of rank 3's twelve items were already gone. The mirror subtree
returns 38 for ranks 4--7. The final application is handed $37 + 38 = 75$ items, has room
for 37, and returns its left input unchanged --- same digest, same 1{,}444 bytes. Rank 3's
single survivor is \texttt{F-3-0}, the first of its twelve, so the result is a strict
prefix of the rank-ordered item list truncated at the budget. This is why
\texttt{rank\_position\_correlation} is $-0.863$: it is not a subtle positional bias but a
cut.

The cell's contribution relative to its siblings is that it shows the truncation is
insensitive to where in the tree it happens. The chain reaches the same 37 identifiers by
folding right-to-left through seven successive applications, thrashing on the way (its
depth-4 and depth-6 merges emit only 300 tokens and 25 items, well under the ceiling, then
recover); the flat cell reaches them at the root and then performs four further merges
that return the identical blob; the $k$-ary cell reaches them in two levels. Four fold
orders, four depths from 2 to 7, one survivor set --- I checked set equality directly and
the symmetric difference is empty. Depth cannot cost fidelity when the last node's budget
is the same as every other node's, because there is no slack for earlier losses to
compound into. These four runs are also the sole input to \texttt{table\_erasure} in
\texttt{make\_tables.py}, which filters to broker-executed incompressible rows, so
\texttt{tab\_erasure} and the macros \texttt{\textbackslash NErasedRanks} (4),
\texttt{\textbackslash NTotalRanks} (8) and \texttt{\textbackslash NErasureCorr} ($-0.86$)
are this campaign and nothing else. One correction the paper's prose needs: it says ``five
of its eight inputs are missing'' where the table says four ranks erased and a fifth
reduced to 8\%.

Of the flagged findings, the imbalance note and the reattachment note are expected for a
binomial tree driven by a shared worker pool, and the model check is the check passing.
The genuinely alarming property of this run is one the findings list cannot flag, because
nothing went wrong: seven merges, seven successes, one attempt each, zero contract
violations, zero hallucinated identifiers, and \texttt{n\_label\_only} is 0 --- every
identifier that survived carried its correct six-digit serial and four-letter checksum, so
\texttt{value\_retention} and \texttt{id\_retention} are both 0.3854 exactly. The operator
was never wrong and never confabulated. It returned a well-formed consolidated report
from which half the population is absent, and no signal anywhere in the protocol
distinguishes that from a complete one.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is real: \texttt{executors} records \texttt{broker}~$\times$~8 and
\texttt{worker}~$\times$~18, so this measures model behaviour and not a caricature, unlike
the \texttt{fid-synth} and \texttt{fid-inc-smoke} cells, whose \texttt{function} executor
implements a drop model by construction. The reading that most needs guarding is
therefore the byte-identity of results across the four cells. This run's final blob is the
same object as the \texttt{flat} cell's last five merges and the \texttt{kary} cell's
level-0 and level-1 results, and the runs shared one persistent pool of Cursor subagents.
I ran the determinism test the contract requires. Two prompt digests are shared across
cells --- \texttt{1586acb2d322} between this run's \texttt{merge:d1} and the chain's, and
\texttt{baffd868ca96} between this run's \texttt{merge:d1} and the flat cell's --- and in
both cases the results differ (294 against 292 tokens, 288 against 289). Identical input
does not give identical output here, so determinism does not explain the identities. But
neither does memory need to: the surviving items are verbatim copies of high-entropy input
strings emitted in rank order, so once the truncation point is fixed at 37 there is
essentially one string that can be returned. Convergence is the parsimonious reading and I
cannot exclude pool memory, but here it would have nothing left to explain.

Second, the 11.81-tokens-per-item constant is measured with \texttt{tokenizer\_exact:
false}, so the estimator is the repository's own approximate counter --- the same counter
the contract check uses, which is what makes the arithmetic self-consistent even if it is
not a real tokeniser's. A different tokeniser would move the cut by an item or two and
would not move the conclusion. The \$0.0500 is likewise modelled from
\$3/Mtok in and \$15/Mtok out, not billed.

Third, this is one trial. The latency comparison against the siblings is safe in direction
because it is mechanical --- three applications on the critical path here against seven
for chain and flat and two for $k$-ary --- and this run's 80.27\,s against
\texttt{kary}'s 51.77\,s is close to the 3:2 ratio the structure predicts. The comparison
against the chain's 634.43\,s is not safe, because the chain's per-call generation times
average 89\,s against this run's 21.8\,s for equivalent prompts; that is a pool-side
difference, not an algorithmic one, and I would not report 7.9$\times$ as a property of
the collective.

Finally, two accounting caveats. \texttt{fact\_retention} scores an identifier as retained
wherever it appears in the result text, and the merge prompt's own instruction line reads
``such as [F-3-2]'' --- an identifier inside the benchmark's own space at $p\ge4$ with
$\ge3$ items per rank. In the surrogate campaigns that line is copied into every
accumulator and inflates the score; here I checked, and rank 3's survivor is
\texttt{F-3-0} rather than \texttt{F-3-2}, so this run is clean. It remains a latent
scoring hazard rather than an error in this measurement. And every \texttt{msg.recv}
records \texttt{admitted: false}, because the collective's internal receives pass
\texttt{admit=False}, so context occupancy is 0\% and this run says nothing at all about
admission pressure despite being the configuration where a root's context should be under
most strain.
