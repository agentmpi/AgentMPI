\begin{abstract}
This is the \texttt{-{}-glossary-op semantic} ablation of the parallel book translation
experiment: eight real Cursor subagents translate one book, and the glossary
\texttt{allreduce} that makes their terminology agree is performed by a model-implemented
operator (\texttt{GLOSSARY\_MERGE}) instead of the exact, associative, idempotent
\texttt{UNION} used by \texttt{real-tr-p8-full}. The measured price of that substitution is
exactly $p-1=7$ extra agent calls, one per internal node of the binomial reduction tree,
carrying 5{,}441 prompt and 4{,}873 completion tokens and costing \$0.0894 --- 95\% of the
\$0.0939 total cost difference against the exact run. The measured benefit is smaller than the
harness's own documentation predicts and different in kind. Replaying \texttt{UNION} over this
run's own eight term sheets, recovered from the blob store, yields a glossary with the
\emph{identical} 203 keys and only 7 differing values (3.4\%): the semantic fold lost nothing,
invented nothing, and never proposed a rendering that no rank had proposed. Two of those seven
differences are the one thing an exact operator provably cannot do --- having chosen one
transliteration of \emph{Adler}, the operator propagated it to \emph{Miss Adler} and
\emph{Miss Irene Adler}, keys \texttt{UNION} treats as unrelated. The predicted population
divergence did not occur and could not have: under \texttt{reduce\_bcast} the root's answer is
broadcast by handle, and the trace shows all seven broadcast messages carrying one digest,
\texttt{c908d2c6e40f}, so all eight ranks hold a byte-identical glossary. The headline
$6.9\times$ wall-time inflation over the exact run is \emph{not} the operator's doing: 742\,s
of this run's 1{,}152.9\,s --- 64\% --- is wall clock during which no rank anywhere was
executing anything, waiting for worker processes to attach, against 2.3\,s of such time in the
exact run. The takeaway is that at $p=8$ on this workload a semantic reduction is a linear-cost,
logarithmic-latency way to buy cross-key consistency that \texttt{UNION} cannot express, and
that its advertised dangers --- information loss compounding with depth, and a population
believing $p$ different glossaries --- were both absent here for reasons that are legible in
the trace and that will not survive a change of algorithm or of scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} does not look like a parallel run. Eight lanes are drawn and the
blue bars in them occupy three narrow islands separated by two long stretches in which nothing
at all is happening. Figure~\ref{fig:concurrency} makes the same point as a step function: one
or two ranks busy from 1.7 to 52.4\,s, six ranks busy for roughly forty seconds around 350--390\,s
decaying to one by 464\,s, a single rank from 910 to 1{,}006\,s, and only then the full eight
from 1{,}007 to 1{,}152\,s. Taking the union of every claim-to-complete interval in the log
gives 395.6\,s of wall clock with at least one rank executing, so 757\,s of the 1{,}152.9\,s
span had no work in flight anywhere in the world. Two windows account for 742.3\,s of that:
52.4\,s\,--\,348.5\,s (296.2\,s) and 464.2\,s\,--\,910.3\,s (446.1\,s).

Those two windows are the run's dominant feature and they are not the semantic operator. The
broker log separates queueing from service exactly: every agent call has a
\texttt{broker.enqueue}, a \texttt{broker.claim}, and a \texttt{broker.complete}. Summing over
all 31 calls gives 2{,}653.6\,s of queue wait against 1{,}398.4\,s of service, so 65.5\% of
total agent-call elapsed time was spent waiting for a worker to pick the task up. In
\texttt{real-tr-p8-full} the same sum is 39.2\,s of queue against 1{,}033.2\,s of service, 3.7\%.
Two stalls dominate. Six of the eight \texttt{terms:u*} calls were enqueued within 30\,ms of the
scatter and not claimed until $t\approx348$--$351$\,s, because ranks 2 through 7 had no worker attached
before then --- the first \texttt{rank.init} with \texttt{executor: worker} for those ranks is at
348.5--351.0\,s, whereas in \texttt{real-tr-p8-full} all eight workers attached within 1.6\,s.
The second stall is \texttt{reduce:GLOSSARY\_MERGE:d2} on rank 0, enqueued at 411.0\,s and not
claimed until 910.3\,s: 499.3\,s of pure queueing on the critical path, in which rank 0 held the
partially folded glossary and every other rank was blocked in the collective behind it. Rank 0's
incarnation counter tells the story --- incarnation 4 attached at 56.4\,s and never claimed
anything, and incarnation 5 did not appear until 910.3\,s.

This has a direct consequence for how the viewer's statistics row should be read. It reports
agent latency p50/p95 of 59.5/388.1\,s and \texttt{metrics.json} gives a maximum of 534.17\,s,
which is the \texttt{reduce:GLOSSARY\_MERGE:d2} call. Only 34.8\,s of that 534.17 was model
service; the remaining 499.3\,s was queue. The \texttt{agent.call} event's \texttt{latency\_s}
field is measured from the harness's point of view --- submission to result --- so on a run where
workers are scarce it is a scheduling metric wearing a model-latency label. The calibration
block inherits the problem: $\alpha_{p99}=389.6$\,s and $\beta=0.0761$\,s/token are fitted to
these inflated latencies, and the $\alpha/\beta$ crossover of 782 tokens should not be believed
for this run.

The second island, 348--464\,s, is where the reduction tree is visible. Six ranks finish their
term sheets almost simultaneously, and then the binomial fold appears as a staircase: rank 2 and
rank 6 each perform a depth-1 merge (410.9\,s and 411.2\,s), rank 4 performs its depth-2 merge
(464.7\,s), and the tree narrows to rank 0. Rank 4's lane in Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is
visibly the widest bar of that island because it did three consecutive agent calls --- its own
term sheet, its depth-1 fold, and its depth-2 fold. The fan-in shape is also legible in
Figure~\ref{fig:comm}: traffic is confined to the binomial edges $0\!\to\!1$, $0\!\to\!2$,
$0\!\to\!4$, $2\!\to\!3$, $4\!\to\!5$, $4\!\to\!6$, $6\!\to\!7$ and their reverses, with the
brightest cell $0\!\to\!4$ at 5{,}644 tokens, most of it the 1{,}540-token merged glossary
travelling down the broadcast tree.

The third island is the run behaving as designed. From 1{,}006.9\,s all eight ranks translate
concurrently, then exchange seam boundaries and revise; Figure~\ref{fig:concurrency} sits at 8
for most of that stretch, and the dip in the middle is the gap between the last translation
completing and the last halo message arriving. The whole productive part of the harness ---
translate, halo exchange, revise, barrier, gather --- takes 146\,s and looks exactly like the
corresponding phase of the exact run.

One asymmetry in the collective table repays attention. The reduce reports \texttt{wall\_s} of
980.70\,s and the broadcast 985.70\,s, which is not two long operations but one: a leaf rank
leaves the reduce in 2\,ms, having only sent, and then blocks inside the broadcast until the
root's answer arrives, so the leaves' waiting is charged to the \texttt{bcast} and the root's
folding to the \texttt{reduce}. Ranks 1, 5, 7 and 3 record \texttt{reduce} walls of 1.4--2.2\,ms
and \texttt{bcast} walls of 985.7, 625.2, 624.2 and 618.7\,s respectively. Summing those
per-rank walls is what produces the 258.7\% coordination share: eight ranks concurrently blocked
in the same 986\,s collective contribute eight times to a total that is then divided by one
wall time.

\section{Interpretation}

\subsection{What is true by construction}

Three of this run's most striking numbers were fixed the moment the configuration was chosen.
The seven extra agent calls relative to \texttt{real-tr-p8-full} are not an empirical finding:
a binary reduction over $p=8$ has $p-1=7$ internal nodes, and \texttt{semantic\_op} builds a
kernel that invokes an agent once per node, so the count is forced. The log confirms the
correspondence exactly --- the seven calls whose \texttt{kind\_label} begins
\texttt{reduce:GLOSSARY\_MERGE} are the only agent calls this run has that
\texttt{real-tr-p8-full} does not, and the other 24 (\texttt{terms}, \texttt{translate},
\texttt{revise}, eight of each) match one for one. The maximum fold depth of 3 is
$\lceil\log_2 8\rceil$, and the labels record the tree level directly: four calls at
\texttt{:d1}, two at \texttt{:d2}, one at \texttt{:d3}. And the byte-identity of the result at
every rank follows from the algorithm rather than from the operator: \texttt{reduce\_bcast}
computes one distinguished answer at the root and ships it, so \texttt{allreduce} correctly
records \texttt{divergence\_risk: false} on all eight of its events even though the underlying
\texttt{coll.reduce} events all carry \texttt{lossy: true}. The two flags mean different things
and the trace keeps them apart properly. The per-rank statistics agree: all eight ranks report
\texttt{glossary\_digest} \texttt{c908d2c6e40f}, and the seven broadcast \texttt{msg.send}
events carry that single digest.

\subsection{What the fold actually did}

The interesting result is what \emph{emerged}, and it is recoverable in full because every
accumulator that crossed a wire was stored in the blob store. The four leaf contributions
travelled as \texttt{msg.send} payloads with an \texttt{acc} field, the four remaining leaf term
sheets and all seven fold outputs are stored as blobs, and matching them by key count and
containment reconstructs the entire tree. Table~\ref{tab:folds} is that reconstruction; the
service times come from \texttt{broker.claim} to \texttt{broker.complete} and the output token
counts from the corresponding \texttt{agent.call} events.

The column that matters is $|\text{out}|$ against $|L\cup R|$. They are equal at every node.
Across all seven folds, at depths 1, 2 and 3, the operator dropped zero keys and invented zero
keys, and the 203 terms in the final glossary are exactly the 203 distinct source terms the
eight ranks proposed between them. For an operator the runtime classifies as lossy
(\texttt{Associativity.APPROX}, hence \texttt{Op.lossy} true, hence \texttt{lossy: true} on
every \texttt{coll.reduce} event), the measured key-level loss on this workload was nil. The
declaration is a worst-case contract, not a measurement, and this run is a data point saying the
worst case did not occur.

Nothing about depth compounded either, and the reason is visible in the token counts. The
operator was not summarising: its output grew with its input at a steady $\approx7.5$ tokens per
term (490 tokens for 62 terms, 809 for 106, 1{,}540 for 203). The budget formula in
\texttt{semantic\_op} gives $\max(300, 1500(1+0.25d))$, so 2{,}625 tokens at depth 3, and the
root fold used 1{,}540 of them --- 59\% headroom. Depth compounds loss for a lossy operator only
when each level forces a re-encoding under pressure; here every level had room to copy, so the
mechanism the module docstring warns about never engaged. That is a scale-conditional result,
not a refutation, and Section~\ref{sec:threats} says where it stops holding.

\subsection{Does the result actually differ from the exact one?}

Yes, and the difference is measurable without leaving this run. Comparing against
\texttt{real-tr-p8-full}'s stored glossary is tempting --- 203 terms versus 206, with 16 keys
unique to the semantic run and 19 unique to the exact one --- but that comparison is worthless,
because the eight term sheets themselves were produced by nondeterministic agent calls and
differ between the two runs. The clean experiment is a within-run counterfactual: take this
run's own eight term sheets from the blob store, fold them with \texttt{agentmpi.ops.UNION}, and
apply the harness's deterministic tiebreak (\texttt{sorted(v)[0]} on each conflict list), which
is precisely what \texttt{real-tr-p8-full} did to its own sheets.

That replay produces 203 keys --- \emph{the identical key set} --- and 7 values that differ from
the semantic result, 3.4\% of the glossary. Exactly five terms attracted conflicting proposals
from the population (\emph{German}, \emph{Irene Adler}, \emph{brougham}, \emph{cabman},
\emph{reasoner}). The semantic operator resolved all five by choosing one of the two renderings
that had actually been proposed and never invented a third, and it chose what \texttt{UNION}'s
lexicographic tiebreak would have chosen on three of the five; \emph{German} (country versus
demonym) and \emph{cabman} (``driver'' versus ``carriage driver'') are the two it decided
differently. The remaining five differences are edits made to terms whose two inputs did not
disagree at all: \emph{Bohemian} and \emph{European} were moved from bare place nouns to
attributive forms, \emph{mistress} from ``kept woman'' to ``lady of the house'', and ---
the interesting pair --- \emph{Miss Adler} and \emph{Miss Irene Adler} were rewritten to use the
transliteration of the surname the operator had already chosen for \emph{Irene Adler}.

That pair is the whole argument for semantic reduction, in miniature and on real data.
\texttt{UNION} resolved \emph{Irene Adler} the same way the model did, but it treats
\emph{Miss Adler} as an unrelated key and left it spelling the surname the other way, because
set union has no notion that two keys are about the same person. The semantic operator noticed
and propagated. Seven agent calls and \$0.0894 therefore bought seven changed glossary entries:
two cross-key consistency repairs an exact operator cannot express, two conflict resolutions
decided against \texttt{UNION}'s arbitrary tiebreak, and three unforced substitutions whose
merit is a matter of translation judgement rather than measurement.

The order-dependence the operator is warned about is also visible, in one instance. Table
\ref{tab:folds} counts six conflicts across the tree but the population only ever disagreed
about five terms; the sixth is \emph{Bohemian} at rank 0's depth-2 fold, and it exists only
because rank 2's depth-1 fold had unilaterally rewritten that term one level earlier. A lossy
operator does not merely resolve the disagreements it is given, it manufactures new ones for the
levels above it, and under a different tree shape that particular conflict would never have
arisen.

The harness's own quality scoring cannot see any of it. Coverage, consistency, divergent
entities, rendering verification, paragraph fidelity and length plausibility are identical
between this run and \texttt{real-tr-p8-full} --- 1.0, 1.0, 0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0 --- and identical
again in \texttt{real-tr-p8-noglossary}, which skips the allreduce entirely. With six canonical
shared entities across eight sections the metric is saturated, so no conclusion about
translation quality can be drawn in either direction from this ablation as scored.

\subsection{The cost, attributed}

Against \texttt{real-tr-p8-full}: agent calls 31 versus 24, prompt tokens 48{,}530 versus
43{,}448, completion tokens 13{,}878 versus 8{,}636, cost \$0.3538 versus \$0.2599, messages 56
in both. Priced at the run's own calibration (\$3/Mtok in, \$15/Mtok out), the seven merge calls
alone account for \$0.0894 of the \$0.0939 difference, so cost attribution to the operator is
essentially exact. Latency is where the naive comparison misleads. The reduce's recorded
\texttt{wall\_s} went from 3.23\,s to 980.70\,s, a factor of 303, and the enclosing allreduce
from 10.24\,s to 985.70\,s, a factor of 96. But rank 0's 980.7\,s inside the reduce decomposes
into 114.2\,s of its own model service, 507.7\,s of broker queue (3.6 + 499.3 + 4.8 across its
three folds), and 358.6\,s spent waiting for rank 2's depth-1 contribution, which was itself
stuck behind rank 2's 348.5\,s wait for a worker. So 866.3\,s --- 88\% --- traces to worker
attachment, directly or through a peer. The operator's own contribution is 224.5\,s of model
service across the seven calls, of which the critical path through the tree,
$\max\!\big(\max(26.4,23.7)+39.0,\ \max(22.8,21.2)+34.8\big)+56.6$, is 122.0\,s. Service time
did grow with depth, roughly in proportion to output size: 23.5\,s mean at depth 1, 36.9\,s at
depth 2, 56.6\,s at depth 3. A well-scheduled version of this run would therefore show a glossary
phase of about two minutes rather than the sixteen recorded, against a genuinely free fold in the
exact run.

\subsection{The flagged findings}

The \finding{warning} on \texttt{halo\_exchange} is a real defect in AgentMPI, and it is not
specific to this configuration. The collective reports zero messages while the fabric logged 14
carrying its internal tag. Inspecting the recorded event shows the payload is
\texttt{\{label, left, right\}} only: no \texttt{algorithm}, \texttt{size}, \texttt{rounds},
\texttt{messages\_sent}, \texttt{tokens\_sent} or \texttt{wall\_s}. The same warning appears in
\texttt{real-tr-p8-full}, \texttt{real-tr-p8-rdallreduce}, \texttt{real-tr-p4-full} and
\texttt{real-tr-p2-full}, and is absent only from \texttt{real-tr-p8-nohalo}, which does not call
the operation. It has since been diagnosed and fixed --- commit \texttt{ee21d79} adds those
fields to all three neighbourhood collectives and asserts on the trace rather than on the runtime
counter --- but the trace analysed here was recorded at commit \texttt{a6f6103}, before the fix,
so the flag is accurate for this artefact and the derived \texttt{halo\_exchange} row in the
collective table (0 messages, 0 tokens, 0\,s, algorithm \texttt{?}) should be read as missing
data rather than as measurement.

The \finding{note} on 15.2\% parallel efficiency and 84.8\% idle rank-seconds is real but
misattributed if read as a property of semantic reduction. The reduction is genuinely serial ---
a $\log_2 p$ tree keeps at most four ranks busy at level 1 and one at level 3 --- but the
efficiency collapse is dominated by the 742.3\,s in which no rank at all was busy. The exact run
with the identical harness scores 77.4\%.

The \finding{note} on a 258.7\% coordination share is an artefact of the metric's definition,
explained above: per-rank collective walls are summed while ranks block concurrently. It should
be read as ``on average each rank spent 2.6 wall times' worth of rank-seconds inside
collectives'', which for eight ranks means roughly a third of the population's time, not more
than all of it.

The two \finding{ok} findings are both correct and both worth keeping. Fifty-six reattachments,
five to eight per rank and reaching incarnation 9 on ranks 0, 1, 4 and 6, is nearly double the 31
of \texttt{real-tr-p8-full}, and it is the same phenomenon as the queue stalls seen from the
other side: each rank role outlived a succession of worker processes, and its mailbox, sequence
numbers and context account survived every handover. Nothing was lost across an attachment
boundary --- 56 sends, 56 receives, zero failures, zero contract violations, zero retries. And
all nine checkable collectives sent exactly the message count their closed forms predict.

\subsection{Position in the ablation ladder}

The harness docstring predicts that a semantic merge ``would be none of those [exact,
associative, commutative, idempotent], and the population would end up believing $p$ slightly
different glossaries''. The first clause remains a correct statement about the operator's
declared algebra; the second did not happen and, under \texttt{reduce\_bcast}, cannot. The
population divergence the docstring warns about is a property of the \emph{algorithm}, not the
operator: \texttt{recursive\_doubling} has every rank perform its own fold sequence, which is why
\texttt{algorithms.allreduce} passes \texttt{divergence\_risk=op.lossy} on that path and a
hardcoded \texttt{False} on the \texttt{reduce\_bcast} path.

The sibling \texttt{real-tr-p8-rdallreduce} does not test that combination. Its configuration is
\texttt{glossary\_op: union} with \texttt{allreduce\_alg: recursive\_doubling}, so its operator is
exact and its 16 \texttt{coll.allreduce} events all record \texttt{divergence\_risk: false} --- as
they should, since recursive doubling with a canonical operand ordering over an exact operator
gives every rank the same answer. No run in \texttt{traces/events/} sets
\texttt{divergence\_risk} true; the semantic-operator-plus-recursive-doubling cell of the design
is empty. This run therefore supplies the cost of a semantic operator and the demonstration that
\texttt{reduce\_bcast} contains it, and says nothing at all about divergence. That cell is where
the project's divergence claim would have to be measured, and the two runs that exist do not
measure it between them.

\section{Threats to this reading}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{The wall-time comparison against \texttt{real-tr-p8-full} is not controlled.} Both runs
executed the same harness against real Cursor subagents, but this one lost 742.3\,s to worker
attachment stalls and the other lost 2.3\,s. Nothing in the configuration explains the
difference; the semantic operator does not spawn workers. Any sentence of the form ``the semantic
glossary made the run $6.9\times$ slower'' is contaminated by an environmental variable neither
run controlled, and the honest operator-attributable latency figure is the 122.0\,s critical path
computed from service intervals. That figure in turn assumes queueing and service are cleanly
separable in the broker log, which they are only if a claimed task begins executing immediately.

\textbf{The zero-loss result is conditional on scale and on budget headroom, and this run cannot
tell which.} The root fold produced 1{,}540 tokens against a 2{,}625-token budget. The glossary
size is set by the book, not by $p$ --- the $p=1$, $p=2$, $p=4$ and $p=8$ union runs report 208,
207, 208 and 206 terms --- so at larger $p$ the same $\approx200$-term payload would pass through
$\lceil\log_2 p\rceil$ folds with a budget that grows only as $1500(1+0.25\lceil\log_2 p\rceil)$.
At $p=8$ that is comfortable. It is also possible that this particular model would have copied
faithfully at any budget and the headroom is irrelevant. One run at one $p$ cannot separate those.

\textbf{The seven-value difference is one sample of a nondeterministic operator.} The whole point
of \texttt{semantic\_op} is that it is not reproducible, and this run folded the tree once. A
second execution over the same eight term sheets would give a different set of edits, possibly a
different count, and possibly a nonzero key loss. The 3.4\% figure is a single draw, not an
expectation, and the run contains no repeat from which to estimate variance.

\textbf{``Better'' is asserted, not measured.} I judged two of the seven edits to be genuine
cross-key consistency repairs and the rest to be plausible translation choices, by reading them.
The harness's quality scorer is saturated --- identical values on every metric for the semantic,
exact and no-glossary runs --- so there is no instrument in this experiment that can distinguish
a semantic merge from no merge at all on output quality. Any claim that the \$0.0894 bought
something is currently a claim about the glossary, not about the book.

\textbf{The counterfactual replay reuses code that has moved on.} The \texttt{UNION} fold I ran
to construct the comparison glossary is today's \texttt{src/agentmpi/ops.py}, whereas the trace
was recorded at commit \texttt{a6f6103}. \texttt{UNION} is a small and stable function and the
\texttt{real-tr-p8-full} glossary it produced at run time is consistent with what today's version
produces, but the replay is a reconstruction rather than a recording, and the seven-value
difference inherits that caveat.

\textbf{Several derived metrics do not mean what they appear to.} Agent latency p95 and maximum
are dominated by broker queueing; the calibration constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are fitted to
those latencies; the coordination share exceeds 100\% by summing concurrent blocking; the
\texttt{halo\_exchange} row is empty because of the instrumentation gap rather than because no
traffic occurred; and \texttt{tokens\_deferred} is reported as 8{,}893 in \texttt{metrics.json}
but 40{,}799 in the \texttt{job.finish} event, the former being the tokens carried by the eight
rendezvous-mode messages and the latter a wider count, so the same name denotes two quantities
depending on where a reader looks. None of these invalidates the operator findings, which rest on
event counts, token counts and blob contents rather than on derived timings, but each is a place
where a reader could take a number from the headline table and be wrong about it.
