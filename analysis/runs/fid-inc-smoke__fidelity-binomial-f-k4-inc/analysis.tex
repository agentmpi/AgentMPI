\begin{abstract}
This is a smoke test: 47 events, 0.062\,s, a \texttt{function} executor on all eight ranks,
run ten minutes before the broker campaign it was checking. Its job was to confirm that the
new \texttt{--incompressible} payload generator and the new value-level retention metrics
plumb through the harness end to end, and it did: the row it produced is the first in the
repository to carry \texttt{n\_intact}, \texttt{n\_label\_only}, \texttt{n\_dropped},
\texttt{value\_retention} and \texttt{id\_retention}, at 96, 0, 0, 1.0 and 1.0. The
structural counts are exact --- \texttt{fold\_depth: 3} $= \lceil\log_2 8\rceil$, 3 rounds,
7 messages $= p-1$, 7 applications, model check passing --- and the retention figure of
1.000 must not be read as a fidelity result. Nothing here could have lost anything: the
surrogate kernel ignores the token budget entirely, so its outputs grow from 386 tokens at
depth 1 to 3{,}033 at the root against a nominal 450, and there is no budget-bound
accumulator for incompressible content to overflow. What little the kernel did discard is
almost comic: the four depth-1 merges all have prompts of exactly 1{,}695 characters, the
kernel seeds its generator from \texttt{seed + len(prompt)}, and replaying its arithmetic
shows all four making the same draw and dropping candidate index 0 --- which is not a fact
but the prompt's own instruction sentence, \texttt{"such as [F-3-2]. Keep every identifier
\dots"}. The smoke test therefore established that the plumbing works and that a surrogate
executor cannot exercise erasure at all. The measurement it cleared the way for is
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc}, which added \texttt{max\_tokens: 450} to the contract, ran on
real agents, and returned 0.3854 with ranks 4 through 7 erased whole.
\end{abstract}

\section{Interpretation}

The structure is by construction and is what a smoke test should verify: depth 3 matching
$\lceil\log_2 8\rceil$, seven messages against a predicted seven, and the finding that all
checkable collectives agree with their closed form is the check passing. What the run adds
to the campaign is a negative result about its own instrument, and it is worth stating
because it is the reason the campaign was re-run on a broker.

Retention 1.000 here has two independent causes and neither is fidelity. The first is that
the surrogate has no notion of a budget: \texttt{\_surrogate\_merge} takes a drop rate and a
seed, never the token ceiling, and the ceiling was in any case advisory in this run --- at
commit \texttt{90ca469} the harness passed the module-level \texttt{MERGE\_CONTRACT}, which
has no \texttt{max\_tokens} field, so the 450 tokens existed only as a sentence in the
prompt and as a \texttt{meta} hint. Whole-rank erasure in this experiment is what happens
when an accumulator that stands for many ranks fills a binding budget; with no binding
budget and no budget-aware operator, the mechanism cannot fire, and \texttt{--incompressible}
--- which removes the operator's ability to re-encode its way out of pressure --- has nothing
to act on. The second cause is the surrogate's actual loss model, which is not the one its
docstring describes. It builds candidates from \texttt{prompt.splitlines()} and keeps lines
matching \texttt{FACT\_ID}, but each input is interpolated as \texttt{json.dumps(\dots)} and
contains no newlines, so a binary merge prompt offers exactly three candidates: the prompt's
instruction line containing the example identifier \texttt{[F-3-2]}, and the two inputs
entire. The unit of loss is a whole input, and the draw is a deterministic function of
prompt character length. Rebuilding the four depth-1 prompts from
\texttt{make\_fact\_report} at the run's seed gives 1{,}695 characters for every one of
them, and the replay keeps candidates 1 and 2 and drops candidate 0 in all four cases ---
the instruction sentence. The stored blob for the $2\to0$ message confirms it: two findings,
24 identifiers, ranks 2 and 3 complete, and no \texttt{[F-3-2]}. So the only thing this run
lost was harness boilerplate, four times, deterministically.

Read against my other fourteen runs, this cell is therefore a plumbing datum and a caution
rather than evidence. The comparison it makes possible is the important one:
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc\_\_fidelity-binomial-f-k4-inc} is the same algorithm at the same
$p$, the same twelve incompressible items per rank and the same nominal 450 tokens, run ten
minutes later, differing in exactly two respects --- a real broker executor, and
\texttt{max\_tokens: 450} in the contract with a semantics clause instructing the operator to
omit whole items rather than re-encode. It retains 37 of 96: ranks 0, 1 and 2 complete, one
item of rank 3, and ranks 4 through 7 gone entirely. That is the measurement the experiment
was built for, and this smoke test is the step that confirmed the new metric block would
record it.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is a surrogate, so nothing here is agent behaviour, and the cost figure of
\$0.1171 is a modelled price for tokens produced by string concatenation. The 0.062\,s wall
time is SQLite writes and event logging.

The replay is a reconstruction rather than a logged fact: a \texttt{function} executor writes
no rows to \texttt{agent\_calls}, so this run's fabric contains no prompts and no results,
only the seven message payloads under \texttt{blobs/}. What is directly observed is that
those payloads contain nested escaped JSON, that their \texttt{findings} arrays have length
2 or 3 rather than 12 or 24, and that the $2\to0$ payload carries 24 identifiers with the
phantom absent. The claim that all four depth-1 prompts were 1{,}695 characters and made
the same draw comes from rebuilding them with the harness's own generator, which reproduces
the observed output-token counts (386 for all four) and the observed blob contents.

Two defects I would report rather than fix. The prompt's example identifier
\texttt{[F-3-2]} is a live identifier that \texttt{fact\_retention} will score if the
operator echoes it; in this run it does no harm, but in
\texttt{fid-synth\_\_fidelity-binomial-f-k4} it is the sole reason rank 3 is credited with
0.25 retention after its real report was destroyed. And the line-based item extraction makes
the surrogate's loss granularity a whole subtree rather than a fact, which means the
$(1-r)^{\text{depth}}$ curve its docstring promises is not what any of these runs exhibit.
