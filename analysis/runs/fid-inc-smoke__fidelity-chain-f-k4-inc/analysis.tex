\begin{abstract}
This is the deep cell of a smoke test: 47 events, 0.197\,s, a \texttt{function} executor on
all eight ranks, run ten minutes before the broker campaign it was clearing the way for. Its
purpose was to confirm that the new \texttt{--incompressible} payload generator and the new
value-level metrics survive a seven-deep fold, and it does --- \texttt{fold\_depth: 7} $=
p-1$, 7 rounds, 7 messages, model check passing, and \texttt{n\_intact: 96},
\texttt{n\_label\_only: 0}, \texttt{n\_dropped: 0}, \texttt{value\_retention: 1.0}. The
retention figure of 1.000 is not a fidelity result, and this cell is the one that shows most
clearly why. The surrogate kernel copies both of its inputs verbatim into its output and
never sees the token budget, so along the chain the outputs run 386, 721, 1{,}231, 2{,}092,
3{,}655, 6{,}602 and 12{,}375 tokens against a nominal ceiling of 450, and the fabric moves
a 6{,}617-token accumulator on the final edge --- 27{,}062 output tokens in total for a fold
over 96 twelve-token facts. Where a real operator under an enforced budget must eventually
discard something, this one simply grows, and \texttt{--incompressible} therefore has no
mechanism to act on: making a payload unshortenable only matters to an operator that is
being squeezed. The single item the kernel did discard, at the first merge, was not a fact
at all but the prompt's own instruction sentence, \texttt{"such as [F-3-2]. Keep every
identifier \dots"}, which the blob for the $6\to5$ message shows absent while both real
reports are intact. What this run establishes, then, is that a surrogate executor cannot
exercise whole-rank erasure, however deep the fold. The erasure measurement is
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc\_\_fidelity-chain-f-k4-inc}: same algorithm, same $p$, same
nominal budget, but enforced and on real agents, and there retention is 0.3854 with ranks 4
through 7 gone.

\section{Interpretation}

The structure is by construction. \texttt{fold\_depth: 7} equals $p-1$, matching the
\texttt{chain} branch of \texttt{algorithms.py}, and this is the deep end of the smoke test's
depth range, which spans 2 (\texttt{kary}), 3 (\texttt{binomial}) and 7 --- three algorithms
rather than four, since \texttt{flat} was omitted from \texttt{--algorithms} for this run.
Seven messages against a predicted seven and seven rounds against a predicted seven are the
model check passing. As a smoke test that is the whole of the positive result: the
incompressible generator produces twelve distinct high-entropy items per rank, the
identifiers survive the plumbing, and the \texttt{value\_retention} block computes.

What the run establishes beyond that is a limitation of the instrument. Whole-rank erasure in
this experiment is what happens when an accumulator standing for many ranks fills a binding
output budget: the later contributions are not compressed but dropped entire. Two things must
be true for that to happen, and neither is true here. The budget is not binding --- at commit
\texttt{90ca469} the harness passed the module-level \texttt{MERGE\_CONTRACT}, which has no
\texttt{max\_tokens} field, so the 450 tokens were a sentence in the prompt and a
\texttt{meta} hint, which is why a 12{,}375-token output records \texttt{attempt: 1} and the
run reports zero contract violations. And the operator is not budget-aware:
\texttt{\_surrogate\_merge} receives a drop rate and a seed and nothing else, so it grows
without limit rather than choosing what to shed. Against that background,
\texttt{--incompressible} is inert. Its point is to close the escape route the broker runs
took --- \texttt{sat-mb-fidelity}'s real agents re-encoded 96 items from 123.6 bytes per
identifier down to 14.6 and then overran the budget by 54\%, so nothing was ever forced out
--- and closing a route the surrogate never used changes nothing.

The kernel's actual loss behaviour is also worth recording, because it is not what its
docstring claims and this cell is a clean instance. It builds candidates from
\texttt{prompt.splitlines()} and keeps lines matching \texttt{FACT\_ID}, but each input is
interpolated as \texttt{json.dumps(\dots)} and contains no newlines, so a binary merge prompt
offers exactly three candidates: the instruction line carrying the example identifier
\texttt{[F-3-2]}, and the two inputs entire. The unit of loss is a whole input, not a fact,
so the docstring's independent per-item drop and its $(1-r)^{\text{depth}}$ curve do not
describe what runs. Here the seven applications produced exactly one discard, at depth 1,
and the blob for the $6\to5$ message identifies it: two findings, 24 identifiers, ranks 6 and
7 complete, phantom absent. Every subsequent payload carries the full prefix --- 24, 37, 49,
60, 72, 84 identifiers, where the odd extra one at depths 2 and 3 is the phantom
\texttt{[F-3-2]}, which survived the draw at rank 5's merge and is then absorbed into rank
3's own set when rank 3 contributes at depth 4 --- so nothing real was lost in seven
applications. Compare the
$p=16$ surrogate chain in \texttt{fid-synth}, where a single unlucky draw at depth 4 took the
whole accumulated tail of ranks 12 to 15 in one application, sixteen items, and the output
collapsed from 862 tokens to 180. That is the shape of this loss model when it does fire: not
a gradient, a cliff.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is a surrogate, so nothing here is agent behaviour. The \$0.4565 is a modelled
price for tokens produced by concatenation, and the 27{,}062 output tokens are the strongest
reason not to pool this run's cost with any broker run's: the real chain at $p=8$ on the same
twelve items per rank emitted 2{,}623 output tokens.

The reconstruction is indirect. A \texttt{function} executor writes no rows to
\texttt{agent\_calls}, so the fabric holds no prompts and no results; I read the seven
message payloads under \texttt{blobs/} and inferred each application's effect by differencing
consecutive identifier sets along the chain. That is sound for the interior but says nothing
about the root's own final application, whose result was never sent --- the claim that rank 0
survived rests on the results file's \texttt{per\_rank\_retention}, not on a blob. I also did
not replay the kernel's draws for this cell as I did for the binomial sibling, where four
prompts of identical length predict the observed discard exactly; here the seven prompt
lengths are all distinct (446 to 6{,}887 tokens), so the single discard is consistent with an
independent draw at $r=0.1$ over seven applications and I have no reason to attribute it to
the length-seeding defect.

Finally, \texttt{value\_retention: 1.0} and \texttt{n\_label\_only: 0} are the metrics whose
first exercise this run is, and a smoke test that returns the trivial answer does not
demonstrate they can return a non-trivial one. That demonstration is
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc}, where \texttt{n\_dropped: 59} and \texttt{n\_label\_only: 0}
together establish that the enforced-budget failure is clean deletion rather than
label-without-payload survival --- which is the distinction the new metric was added to make.
