\begin{abstract}
This is the deepest cell of the enforced, incompressible reduction: a serial left fold over
eight ranks, seven successive applications, \texttt{fold\_depth: 7}. It reaches the same
0.385 retention and the same 37 survivors as its depth-2, depth-3 and depth-7 siblings ---
I compared the identifier sets directly and the symmetric difference is empty --- which is
the campaign's central negative result about tree shape. What this cell contributes that
none of the others can is the shape of the loss along the way, because the fold advances
one rank per hop and every intermediate is in the store. The chain is a clean prefix
truncation at every step: at depth 2 it keeps ranks 5 and 6 whole and shaves rank 7 to ten
items of twelve; at depth 3 it keeps 4, 5, 6 whole and shaves rank 7 to two; at depth 4 it
keeps 3 and 4 whole and shaves rank 5 to one. The reduction never distributes its loss and
never corrupts a payload; it writes items in rank order until it stops. And it stops too
early. The applications that are forced to drop emit only 300 tokens and 25 items against a
450-token ceiling that holds 38, so a third of the available budget goes unused and the
loss is worse than the arithmetic requires. The 0.385 endpoint is therefore reached by two
different mechanisms in this campaign --- a budget-saturating truncation in the tree cells
and an undershooting one here --- which agree only because 25 surviving items plus rank 0's
twelve happens to equal the ceiling exactly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is a descending staircase and nothing else. Rank 7 emits one
tick at $t = 0.01$\,s and never appears again; rank 6 holds a bar, hands off to rank 5,
which hands to rank 4, and so on up to rank 0, whose bar ends at 634.44\,s. Exactly one
rank is busy at any instant --- peak ranks busy 1 of 8, serial fraction of active time
100\% --- which is the algorithm's definition rather than a defect: rank $r$ blocks in
\texttt{\_crecv} until rank $r+1$ delivers its accumulator, folds its own payload in as the
left operand so that evaluation order is exactly rank order, and sends onward. The
\texttt{coordination\_share} of 53.3\% is the highest of the four cells for the same
reason: seven ranks spend most of the run blocked inside the collective, and those
rank-seconds are counted, where the flat cell's seven idle ranks return in milliseconds
and contribute nothing to the same statistic. Load imbalance of 2.07$\times$ is the
flattest in the campaign, and is the one respect in which the chain looks good: it spreads
its work over seven ranks instead of one.

The bars are wildly uneven and this is the run's main measurement trap. The seven merges
took 94.52, 77.86, 157.38, 36.78, 161.63, 27.76 and 68.99\,s, a 5.8$\times$ spread, on
prompts of 446, 577, 691, 735, 585, 723 and 585 tokens --- a range of 1.6$\times$ with no
correlation to latency. Almost none of this is queueing: differencing every
\texttt{broker.enqueue}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.claim} pair in the log gives 7.1\,s of total
claim wait against 624.9\,s of generation, so the 634\,s is real model time. But it is
real model time at 89.3\,s per call against the binomial sibling's 21.8\,s for prompts of
the same size and outputs of the same size, which is a property of the worker pool at the
moment this cell ran, not of the collective. The mechanical prediction is that seven serial
applications cost 3.5 times what the $k$-ary tree's two cost; the measured ratio is
12.3$\times$, and the difference between those two numbers is pool-side. The log runs to
1001\,s, 367\,s past \texttt{job.finish}, with 47 reattachments and 24 trailing events, and
the dashboard's health table shows all eight ranks red and suspected \texttt{fail\_stop}
for that reason while \texttt{metrics.json} records \texttt{ok: true} and no failures.

The communication matrix, Figure~\ref{fig:comm}, is the chain's superdiagonal: seven edges
$7\!\to\!6$, $6\!\to\!5$, \dots, $1\!\to\!0$ carrying 173, 307, 421, 464, 315, 453 and 315
tokens, 2{,}448 in total --- the most fabric traffic of the four cells, because every hop
ships an accumulator rather than a leaf. The weights are the fidelity story in profile:
they rise from 173 to 464 as the accumulator fills and then oscillate between 315 and 453
instead of continuing to rise. An accumulator that stops growing at hop four of seven is
an accumulator that has stopped accumulating.

\section{Interpretation}

The structure is by construction and the checks pass: seven messages against a closed form
of $p-1=7$ and seven rounds against $p-1=7$, so \texttt{model\_checks\_agree} is 1 of 1
and Figure~\ref{fig:collectives} shows no red diamond. The regime must be established
before anything is read into the retention figure, and the fabric settles it. All seven
\texttt{agent\_calls} rows carry \texttt{contract} with \texttt{max\_tokens: 450} and the
non-empty semantics clause instructing the operator to ``omit whole items rather than
abbreviating or re-encoding any''. This is the enforced regime: the runtime counts the
returned tokens, rejects an overrun and retries. The \texttt{sat-mb-fidelity} chain ran the
same $p$, the same twelve items per rank and the same nominal 450-token budget with
\texttt{max\_tokens: null}, emitted 695 tokens at the root, and scored 1.000. The
provenance commit \texttt{f121647} does not contain \texttt{merge\_contract}; the enforced
version was committed two minutes after this campaign wrote its results as
\texttt{561d8a17}, so the commit field records HEAD at launch and the fabric is the
authority on what actually ran.

What emerged is the fold's internal shape, and it separates two things the aggregate
conflates. Reconstructing every intermediate from the store gives per-rank item counts of
$\{6\!:\!12, 7\!:\!12\}$ at depth 1; $\{5\!:\!12, 6\!:\!12, 7\!:\!10\}$ at depth 2;
$\{4\!:\!12, 5\!:\!12, 6\!:\!12, 7\!:\!2\}$ at depth 3;
$\{3\!:\!12, 4\!:\!12, 5\!:\!1\}$ at depth 4;
$\{2\!:\!12, 3\!:\!12, 4\!:\!12, 5\!:\!1\}$ at depth 5;
$\{1\!:\!12, 2\!:\!12, 3\!:\!1\}$ at depth 6; and
$\{0\!:\!12, 1\!:\!12, 2\!:\!12, 3\!:\!1\}$ at depth 7. Every one of those is a strict
prefix of the rank-ordered item list: the lowest-numbered ranks present in the input are
kept whole, the tail is shaved, and nothing in between is thinned. That is
whole-rank-plus-partial-tail erasure rather than under-representation, it explains
\texttt{rank\_position\_correlation} $=-0.863$, and it is a direct consequence of two
independent decisions --- the operator writes items in the order it reads them, and
\texttt{reduce} makes the local payload the left operand so that a non-commutative operator
sees rank order. Also worth recording: \texttt{n\_label\_only} is 0, so
\texttt{value\_retention} and \texttt{id\_retention} are both 0.3854 exactly. Every
identifier that survived carried its correct six-digit serial and four-letter checksum.
The operator is honest; it simply stops.

Where this cell disagrees with its siblings is on whether the budget explains the loss, and
the disagreement is the most useful thing here. An incompressible item is
\texttt{[F-0-0] serial 597623 checksum LVPG}: nine tokens of content, 11.81 tokens once
quoted into the JSON array, so a 450-token ceiling holds 38.1 items. The tree cells sit
exactly there --- 437 tokens for 37 items, 449 for 38 --- and their loss is the ceiling.
This run's outputs oscillate: 449 tokens and 38 items when the input fits, then 300 tokens
and 25 items when it does not, then 438 and 37, then 300 and 25, then 438 and 37. The
applications that are forced to drop undershoot the ceiling by a third and delete about
thirteen items more than they had to. So the chain's loss is partly the budget and partly
the operator being more conservative than instructed, and the two cells' agreement on 0.385
is arithmetic coincidence: the last application here received 25 + 12 = 37 items, which
fits, so it lost nothing, and 37 is also where the tree cells' ceiling lands. Read
carefully, this strengthens rather than weakens the campaign's conclusion --- depth did not
cost this run anything even though it had seven chances to compound loss --- but it means
``the same 37 survivors under every algorithm'' should not be read as a single mechanism
operating four times.

Of the flagged findings, the serial-execution warning is the algorithm's definition, the
2.07$\times$ imbalance is a note and is genuinely the best in the campaign, and the 47
reattachments up to incarnation 13 are the broker's normal mode under a shared pool. None
is a defect. This cell's contribution relative to its siblings is to bound the cost of
depth: seven applications on the critical path, 634.43\,s against the $k$-ary tree's
51.77\,s and the binomial tree's 80.27\,s, for exactly the same 37 items and the same
2{,}623 output tokens. It is the run in the campaign whose result most cleanly says that
nothing is bought by folding deeply.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is real --- \texttt{executors} records \texttt{broker}~$\times$~8 and
\texttt{worker}~$\times$~47 --- so this is model behaviour, not a surrogate implementing a
loss model by construction as \texttt{fid-synth} and \texttt{fid-inc-smoke} do. The
latency claim is the one I would most want to withdraw. With one trial per algorithm I
cannot separate the chain's 89.3\,s mean call latency from run-to-run variability in the
pool, and this cell's own calls range over 27.76--161.63\,s on near-identical prompts, so
the within-run standard deviation is already large enough to make a between-run comparison
unsafe. Treat the direction as established by mechanism --- seven serial applications
cannot be faster than two --- and the magnitude, 12.3$\times$ against $k$-ary, as an
overstatement of roughly 3.5$\times$ worth of structure plus an unknown amount of pool
weather.

Second, the intermediate reconstruction depends on the accumulators in
\texttt{runs/inc-mb-fidelity-inc/fidelity-chain-f-k4-inc/blobs/} being the objects the
operator actually saw, which they are: each is the \texttt{result\_digest} of one
\texttt{agent\_calls} row and the \texttt{digest} of the corresponding \texttt{msg.send},
so the chain of custody from one application to the next is in the fabric. Unlike its
siblings this cell shares no result digest with any other run in the campaign --- its fold
order is the reverse of the flat cell's and its intermediates are disjoint from the tree
cells' --- which incidentally removes the shared-pool convergence question here almost
entirely. The one shared prompt digest, \texttt{1586acb2d322} between this run's
\texttt{merge:d1} and the binomial cell's, returned different results (292 against 294
tokens), so the executor is demonstrably non-deterministic and no identity in this campaign
can be explained by replay.

Third, the 11.81-tokens-per-item constant comes from the repository's approximate counter
with \texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}. That is the same counter the contract check uses, so
the arithmetic is internally consistent, but the ``38 items fit'' figure is not a claim
about any production tokeniser. The \$0.0524 is modelled from \$3/Mtok in and \$15/Mtok
out. And \texttt{fact\_retention} counts an identifier wherever it occurs in the result
text, including the merge prompt's own instruction line ``such as [F-3-2]'', which lies
inside the benchmark's identifier space; I checked, and rank 3's survivor here is
\texttt{F-3-0} at every depth, so no phantom inflates this run --- but it does inflate the
surrogate campaigns and the metric should exclude the template.
