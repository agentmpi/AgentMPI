\begin{abstract}
This is the extreme corner of a ten-cell transport sweep and its single strongest
result. Eight agent-free ranks ring-exchange a 32{,}768-word payload under
\texttt{Mode.RENDEZVOUS} --- 43{,}116 estimated tokens, which is $1.32\times$ the
entire 32{,}768-token context budget of the rank receiving it. The run completed: 34
events, 0.08\,s, all eight messages delivered, zero admissions, a context high water
of zero and 0\% occupancy on every rank, with 344{,}928 tokens deferred. A payload
larger than a receiver's whole context window crossed the ring without occupying any
of it. The eager cell at the same size produced 18 events, no messages and
\texttt{ERR\_CONTEXT\_OVERFLOW} on all eight ranks, and it is over both limits at
once: $10.5\times$ the 4{,}096-token unexpected-message credit that actually refused
it, and $1.32\times$ the context budget, so it could not have succeeded under any
credit setting. That asymmetry is the whole of AgentMPI's context-safety claim in one
pair of runs, and this is the half that works.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows eight lanes with two grey lifecycle diamonds and two
green message ticks each and no bars, which is a property of an agent-free benchmark:
the executor is \texttt{none} on all eight ranks, there were zero agent calls, and no
\texttt{task.*} event was emitted, so ``idle fraction 100.0\%'' and ``achieved
parallelism 0.00$\times$'' say that no rank held a task rather than that ranks
waited. This is the longest-spanning run in the sweep at 75\,ms, with a 55\,ms rank
start-up window and receive latencies of 37\,ms median and 53\,ms maximum, an order
of magnitude above the 2.5\,ms median at 128 words. None of that is the payload
arriving: a rendezvous receive transfers nothing, and it is the ring's own ordering
that inflates the figure. Every rank sends before it receives, and
\texttt{Communicator.send} calls \texttt{fabric.blobs.put} before it does anything
else, so each rank writes 163{,}840 bytes to the content store before it looks in its
mailbox --- and that write sits inside the latency of the message already waiting
there. The eager cell at this size, which sent nothing at all, spans 70\,ms for the
same reason: its blob was written and only then was the send refused. Wall time in
this sweep tracks bytes into the content store, not transport mode.

Figure~\ref{fig:comm} shows the ring as one off-diagonal band of 43{,}116 tokens per
edge, rank $r$ to rank $(r{+}1) \bmod 8$. The eager cell at this size has no such
figure, because there is no traffic to plot. The row that carries the result is in
the rank health table: 0\% context occupancy on all eight ranks, with every
\texttt{msg.recv} recording \texttt{admitted: false} and \texttt{admitted\_tokens: 0}
against \texttt{tokens: 43116}, and every \texttt{rank.finalize} recording
\texttt{admissions: 0}, \texttt{n\_items: 0}, \texttt{high\_water: 0}, \texttt{used:
0}. The stats row reads \texttt{TOKENS DEFERRED 344.9k}. Set against the eager
column, where occupancy climbs 1\%, 2\%, 8\% across 128, 512 and 2{,}048 words and
then stops because the runs stop, the rendezvous column is a flat zero from 128 words
to 32{,}768 --- a factor of 256 in payload with no change in the receiver's context
at all.

\section{Interpretation}

By construction: the ring, eight ranks, one send and one receive each, and the mode.
Note that the mode here is what AgentMPI would have chosen unprompted. Every
\texttt{rank.init} records \texttt{eager\_limit: 1000000000}, disabling
\texttt{\_choose\_mode}'s threshold, but the shipped default is
\texttt{DEFAULT\_EAGER\_LIMIT} = 2048 tokens and this payload is 43{,}116, so the
library's own policy is rendezvous by a factor of twenty-one. The eager cell at this
size is the harness overriding that policy, and the override does not merely
under-perform --- it cannot work.

The reason it cannot work is the point of this cell, and it is a stronger statement
than the one available one size down. At 8{,}192 words the eager payload is 10{,}779
tokens: over the 4{,}096-token unexpected-message credit but comfortably inside the
32{,}768-token context budget, so that pair isolates a flow-control ceiling and shows
AgentMPI refusing a message the receiver could actually have held. Here the payload
is 43{,}116 tokens, which exceeds the receiver's entire context window. Even with
unlimited eager credit the exchange is impossible: the message would have arrived,
\texttt{\_deliver} would have called \texttt{rt.admit}, and
\texttt{ContextAccount.admit} would have found \texttt{would\_fit} false against a
budget of 32{,}768 with \texttt{strict\_context} true, failing with
\texttt{ERR\_TRUNCATE} instead of \texttt{ERR\_CONTEXT\_OVERFLOW}. There is no
configuration of the eager path that delivers this payload, because eager's contract
is that the payload enters the receiver's context and the payload does not fit. And
yet the artefact moved: all eight envelopes were delivered, the blob
\texttt{04af1d57ed51} sits in the fabric at its full 163{,}840 bytes, byte-identical
to the one the failed eager run wrote, and any receiver could have called
\texttt{fetch} with a \texttt{View} to take a projection of it that did fit. This is
what \texttt{comm.py} means when it says MPI hides the eager/rendezvous choice because
both modes deliver the same bytes while AgentMPI exposes it because they do not have
the same effect on the receiving agent's context. The bytes were the same in both runs.
The effect was total.

The deferred figure needs a correction, and this cell shows it at its largest.
\texttt{metrics.json} reports 344{,}928, which is $8 \times 43{,}116$: one rendezvous
send per rank, exactly equal to \texttt{tokens sent}, as it must be when every message
is rendezvous, and $4.00\times$ the 8{,}192-word cell for $4\times$ the payload. The
results file records 689{,}856, and the per-rank events say why ---
\texttt{tokens\_deferred: 86232} against \texttt{tokens\_sent: 43116}, a rank
deferring twice what it sent, which is impossible for a quantity documented as a
subset of transmitted traffic. Until commit \texttt{0fea51d2},
\texttt{RankCost.tokens\_deferred} accumulated on rendezvous sends and on every
non-admitted receive alike, so each rank charged its own send and its predecessor's
arrival to one counter. The fix separated \texttt{tokens\_deferred} (send-side,
rendezvous only) from \texttt{tokens\_unadmitted} (receive-side, transport-agnostic),
under which this run reports 344{,}928 of each. \texttt{cost.summarise} reconstructs
from the log and was always correct, so the viewer and \texttt{metrics.json} already
show the right number; only the archived results carry the doubled one. The fix's
summary reasons that the paper's transport table is unaffected because the microbench
admits its receives --- correct for the eager rows, which read zero, and incorrect for
the rendezvous rows, because \texttt{bench\_transport} passes no \texttt{admit}
argument and \texttt{\_deliver} defaults it to \texttt{False} for rendezvous. All five
rendezvous entries in that table are exactly $2\times$ the send-side deferral.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The headline --- 43{,}116 tokens moved into a 32{,}768-token receiver at zero cost ---
is true and incomplete, and the incompleteness is the main threat. Nothing in this
sweep calls \texttt{fetch}. The receivers finished holding a digest, a token count, a
kind and a one-line synopsis of a document none of them read, so what is demonstrated
is that a large artefact can be \emph{addressed} without consuming context, not that
it can be \emph{used} without consuming context. A rank that needed the whole thing
would find it does not fit and would have to project it through a \texttt{View} or
release something first, and no cell in this sweep exercises either. The claim that
eager could not have worked at any credit setting is a code-path argument from
\texttt{rank.py}, not a measurement --- it was not run --- although the arithmetic
behind it, 43{,}116 against 32{,}768, is not in question. Token counts are estimates:
provenance records \texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}, and 43{,}116 is
$\lceil 163840/3.8 \rfloor$ from a character heuristic applied to one repeated word.
A real BPE tokeniser would price this text near 32{,}768 tokens, which would put it
essentially \emph{at} the context budget rather than 32\% above it, weakening the
second-ceiling argument to a marginal case while leaving the first ceiling --- an
order of magnitude above the credit either way --- untouched. Both budgets are
benchmark parameters rather than library defaults (\texttt{unexpected\_limit} 4{,}096
against a shipped $8\times$ eager limit; \texttt{context\_budget} 32{,}768 against
128{,}000), so the ratio between payload and window is one the benchmark chose. And
the executor is \texttt{none} with zero agent calls, so the 0\% occupancy is an entry
in \texttt{ContextAccount} rather than an observation about a model, while the 75\,ms
wall time is one sample of eight SQLite-backed rank start-ups and eight 160\,KiB blob
writes.
