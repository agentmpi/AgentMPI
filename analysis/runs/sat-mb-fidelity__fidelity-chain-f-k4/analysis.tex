\begin{abstract}
This is the deepest cell of the saturation attempt --- seven successive re-summarisations
of an accumulator that must eventually carry ninety-six items --- and it is the cell in
which the campaign's failure to saturate is most completely explained. Retention is 1.000,
all eight ranks intact, no fabricated identifiers, and the reason is visible in the seven
merge outputs recovered from the fabric: 490, 387, 505, 634, 531, 613 and 695 tokens for
accumulators of 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72 and 84 items, against a stated budget of 450. Six of
the seven exceeded it, the last by 54\%, and every one recorded \texttt{attempt: 1}, because
the contract stored in this run's fabric reads \texttt{max\_tokens: null} with an empty
semantics string while \texttt{meta} carries \texttt{\{"max\_tokens": 450\}}. The budget
reached the worker as a generation hint and never reached the checker. Alongside the
overrun, the operator re-encoded hard: bytes per identifier in the transmitted accumulator
fall 123.6, 68.5, 26.8, 25.9, 21.2, 15.8, 14.6 as the fold proceeds, an 8.5$\times$
compression that converges on a packed table (\texttt{[F-1-0]107n [F-1-1]108d \dots}). An
operator permitted both to compress and to overflow is never forced to discard anything,
which is why depth 7 scores exactly what depth 2 scores in this campaign, and why the
tree-shape hypothesis cannot be tested here at all. The remedy was applied ten minutes of
wall-clock later: \texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc} enforces \texttt{max\_tokens: 450} on
incompressible payloads, and its chain cell --- same algorithm, same $p$, same nominal
budget --- retains 0.3854 with ranks 4 through 7 erased whole. One knob separates the two.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is a staircase running from rank 7 down to rank 0, one bar per
lane, which is the chain reduction drawn literally: rank 7 sends its leaf report to rank 6,
which merges and sends to rank 5, and so on to the root, so exactly one rank is ever busy
and the critical path is the whole run. Peak ranks busy is 1 of 8 and the serial fraction of
active time is 100\%. Two features of the picture are not the algorithm, however, and they
dominate it. The first is the enormous gap between rank 6's bar, which ends at $t=26$\,s,
and rank 5's, which does not begin until $t=934$\,s. Differencing every
\texttt{broker.enqueue}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.claim}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.complete}
triple in the log localises it precisely: rank 5's \texttt{merge:d2} sat enqueued for
907.71\,s before any worker claimed it, and rank 1's \texttt{merge:d6} for a further
188.35\,s. Summing the seven waits gives 1{,}100.32\,s of the 1{,}376.99\,s to
\texttt{job.finish} --- 79.9\% of the run --- against 273.82\,s of claim-to-complete
generation, which matches \texttt{busy\_union\_s} of 273.102 to within the event
granularity. The second is the 49 reattachments and the 57 \texttt{rank.init} events for
eight ranks: the pool churned throughout, and the log continues to 1{,}682.14\,s past
\texttt{job.finish}, which is why the dashboard's rank health table shows every rank red
and suspected \texttt{fail\_stop} while \texttt{metrics.json} records \texttt{ok: true},
zero failures and zero contract violations.

This makes the run's headline wall time unusable as an algorithmic measurement, and it is
worth saying plainly because the four cells of this campaign are otherwise directly
comparable: 1{,}376.98\,s here against the flat fold's 197.62\,s, the binomial tree's
77.56\,s and the $k$-ary tree's 53.07\,s. The structural prediction for chain against
binomial at $p=8$ is $7/3 = 2.33\times$; the measurement is 17.8$\times$. Take the queue
wait out and the seven merges cost 273.8\,s, giving 3.5$\times$, which is the figure I would
defend. It is also the origin of the flagged calibration warning: the regression returned
$\beta = -2.0594$\,s per token on $R^2 = 0.392$, latency apparently falling as output grew,
because the two longest-latency calls are the two that waited in the queue and the wait
enters the latency without entering the token count. The guard was right to reject the fit,
and \texttt{alpha\_p50} of 47.5\,s is a median, not a model.

One metric does not survive contact with the log. \texttt{max\_claim\_wait\_s} reads 0.0
for a run containing a 907-second enqueue-to-claim interval, because the metric is computed
from \texttt{broker.expire} events --- waits that ended in a timeout --- rather than from
the enqueue-to-claim difference for calls that were eventually claimed. Every call here was
claimed and completed, so nothing expired, and the single most consequential quantity in
the run is reported as zero. I would report this as a real gap in the instrumentation
rather than a property of the run: \texttt{coordination\_share} of 61.9\% is the only
metric that notices anything, and it notices it for the wrong reason, since it counts the
downstream ranks' blocked receives rather than the broker starvation that caused them.

\section{Interpretation}

What the campaign varied is legible from the config and it is not what the \texttt{sat-}
prefix suggests was achieved. Against \texttt{real-mb-fidelity} --- same $p$, same broker
executor, same three algorithms plus \texttt{kary} --- this campaign raised \texttt{facts}
from 4 to 12 and lowered \texttt{merge\_budget} from 900 to 450. The root must therefore
render 96 items into half the space the baseline had for 32. On the baseline's own
encoding, roughly 19 tokens per item, 450 tokens holds about 23 items, so the fold should
have saturated after two ranks and stayed saturated for five more. It did not saturate at
all, in any of the four cells, and this one shows both escape routes in a single monotone
sequence. The operator re-encodes: rank 7's leaf report spends 1{,}483 bytes on 12 items
and the accumulator arriving at the root spends 1{,}225 bytes on 84, with the intermediate
forms visibly abandoning prose for a packed table --- \texttt{[F-3-0] 121 nom} at depth 4,
then \texttt{[F-2-0] 114n [F-2-1] 115d \dots} at depth 5, where the \texttt{findings} array
collapses from 60 strings to 6 because a dozen items are packed into each. And when
re-encoding runs out, the operator simply exceeds the number: 490 tokens at the first
merge, already 9\% over, and 695 at the last, 54\% over. The prompt itself licenses the
first route (``You may compress wording, but you may not drop an item'') and the run's
plumbing licenses the second. Saturation was never reached, so this campaign contributes a
negative result about the design rather than a measurement of retention under pressure, and
that negative result is what motivated the two changes visible in the repository at
\texttt{HEAD}: \texttt{merge\_contract(budget)}, which puts \texttt{max\_tokens} in the
contract with a semantics clause telling the operator to omit whole items rather than
re-encode, and \texttt{--incompressible}, which removes the re-encoding route by making the
payload high-entropy.

The structural numbers are true by construction and both model checks pass: seven messages
against $p-1 = 7$, seven rounds against the closed form, \texttt{fold\_depth: 7}. This cell
therefore establishes the reference point at the deep end of the campaign's depth range,
which spans 2 (\texttt{kary}), 3 (\texttt{binomial}) and 7 (\texttt{chain} and
\texttt{flat}) --- three distinct depths across four algorithms, because \texttt{flat} is
not a one-shot $p$-way fold: \texttt{algorithms.py} applies the binary kernel $p-1$ times
at the root, so it too has \texttt{fold\_depth: 7}, with \texttt{rounds: 1}. That all four
score retention 1.000 with identical per-rank vectors is the campaign's whole result, and
under an unenforced budget it is uninformative about the depth question rather than
evidence against it. The informative version of the same comparison is in
\texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc}, where all four cells again agree exactly, this time at 0.3854
with the same 37 survivors; there the agreement does refute the hypothesis, because the
budget is binding.

Of the flagged findings, the serial-execution warning and the 2.06$\times$ imbalance are the
algorithm's definition rather than defects --- 2.06 is in fact the mildest imbalance in the
campaign, which is an artefact of the queue wait inflating the mean rather than a virtue of
the chain. The 49 reattachments are the broker pool's normal mode. The calibration warning
is a genuine warning about this run's numbers, correctly raised. The model-check finding is
the check passing. What is not flagged, and should be, is the queue starvation.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is real --- \texttt{broker}~$\times$~8 and \texttt{worker}~$\times$~49 --- so
this is model behaviour, and it must not be pooled with the \texttt{function}-executor
cells in \texttt{fid-synth} and \texttt{fid-inc-smoke}, whose operator drops whole
accumulator blobs at a fixed probability and therefore implements a loss model rather than
exhibiting one.

The most serious threat is that retention 1.000 does not measure information transport.
\texttt{make\_fact\_report} builds item $i$ of rank $r$ with throughput $100 + 7r + i$ under
workload \texttt{['nominal','degraded','saturated'][i \% 3]}, so the payload is a
deterministic function of the identifier, and the packed encoding this run converges on
reproduces the rule literally: \texttt{[F-1-0]107n [F-1-1]108d [F-1-2]109s} is $107, 108,
109$ with the workload initial. A rank whose message had been lost entirely could be
reconstructed by an operator that had merely noticed the pattern, and it would score as
retained. The chain is the cell where this bites hardest, because its accumulator is a
strictly increasing rank prefix in an arithmetic sequence and each merge sees the next rank
only as the continuation of a series it has already seen six times. This is precisely why
\texttt{--incompressible} exists, and why the value-level metrics
(\texttt{n\_intact}, \texttt{n\_label\_only}, \texttt{value\_retention}) are reported only
for the \texttt{-inc} campaigns: here I can verify that 96 identifiers arrived, not that 96
facts did.

Second, single trial, and one whose timing is contaminated as described. Per-call
generation latencies were 25.15, 46.67, 22.47, 32.87, 45.87, 30.27 and 70.52\,s on prompts
of 830--1{,}077 tokens, a 3.1$\times$ spread with no relation to prompt size, so any
per-merge cost I quote is a single draw from a wide distribution. The 8.5$\times$
compression figure is likewise one operator's choices on one pass; the sibling cells'
depth-1 merges at the same prompt structure returned between 211 and 491 tokens, so
per-item cost in the compressible regime is a decision made afresh at each application and
not a constant of the configuration.

Third, the four cells of this campaign ran sequentially against one persistent pool of
Cursor subagents, so cross-run text reuse was mechanically possible, and two of this
campaign's late accumulators are byte-identical across cells. I ran the determinism test
the contract requires: prompt digest \texttt{edf28c1217be} appears in both this campaign's
\texttt{flat} and \texttt{binomial} cells and returns different results (354 against 211
tokens, different digests), so identical input does not give identical output here and
determinism cannot explain the identities. Convergence on the canonical packed encoding
can, and is consistent with the identities occurring only among the dense late
accumulators, but pool memory cannot be excluded from the trace.

Finally the accounting caveats. Every \texttt{msg.recv} records \texttt{admitted: false}
because the collective's internal receives pass \texttt{admit=False}, so context occupancy
reads 0\% on ranks that consumed up to 1{,}077 prompt tokens and this run says nothing
about admission pressure. The \$0.0782 is modelled from \$3/Mtok in and \$15/Mtok out with
\texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}, not billed, and the same approximate counter produced the
490/695-token measurements, so ``54\% over budget'' is internally consistent rather than
externally exact. And \texttt{rank\_position\_correlation: 0.000} is not a fairness result
but an undefined quantity: at retention 1.000 the per-rank variance is zero and the code
returns 0.0 through a guard.
