\begin{abstract}
This is the binomial-tree cell of the saturation attempt, and it is the run that supplies
the paper's ``at equal quality the tree wins'' number: \texttt{\textbackslash NFidTree} is
78, and it is this run's 77.56\,s. Eight ranks contribute twelve uniquely identified items
each into a 450-token merge budget --- three times the items and half the budget of the
\texttt{real-mb-fidelity} baseline, which is what makes this a saturation study --- and it
does not saturate. Retention is 1.000, all 96 identifiers reach the root, none is
fabricated. The store shows why, and the answer is not that 96 items fit: the operator
re-encoded, converging on a packed notation that carries an item in 14.3 bytes at the root
where its own depth-1 output spent 57.7, and then it overran, emitting 698 tokens against
a stated 450. The contract in this run's fabric records \texttt{max\_tokens: null} with an
empty semantics string against \texttt{meta} of \texttt{\{"max\_tokens": 450\}}, so this is
the advisory regime and the budget was never checked. The measurement I find most useful
here is one the aggregate hides. This run performs four structurally identical depth-1
merges: same algorithm, same depth, same 933-token prompt, 24 items in and 24 items out
every time. Their outputs are 211, 269, 356 and 491 tokens --- a 2.3$\times$ spread in how
long an operator chooses to be when nothing about its task has changed. Any arithmetic that
predicts saturation from a per-item token cost has to survive that variance, and in the
compressible regime it does not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is the textbook binomial tree in three bands, and it is the only
cell in this campaign with substantial concurrency. Ranks 0, 2, 4 and 6 all hold bars
together from about $t=1$\,s to $t=22$\,s; ranks 0 and 4 hold bars from about $t=25$\,s to
$t=48$\,s; rank 0 holds the last bar alone to 77.56\,s. Peak ranks busy is 4 of 8, achieved
parallelism 1.86$\times$, and the serial fraction of active time is 49.2\% --- half the run
is the necessarily serial upper levels. Ranks 1, 3, 5 and 7 emit a tick each inside the
first 40\,ms and then nothing: they are the tree's leaves, their \texttt{coll.reduce}
records \texttt{fold\_depth: 0}, and their idleness is why load imbalance reads
3.54$\times$ and 76.8\% of purchased rank-seconds went unused. Both are structural at $p=8$
and neither is a defect.

The bar widths are strikingly uniform and the outputs behind them are not. Seven merges at
21.00, 17.03, 22.15, 19.96, 20.06, 20.02 and 23.85\,s --- a range of 1.4$\times$ --- on
prompts of 933, 933, 933, 933, 887, 694 and 1{,}005 tokens, and outputs of 491, 269, 356,
211, 505, 373 and 698. Latency is nearly constant while output length varies by
2.3$\times$, which says the per-call cost here is dominated by a fixed overhead rather than
by generation volume; the calibration block agrees, fitting $\alpha = 21.2$\,s with a
crossover at 2{,}788 tokens, far above anything this run produced. Of the run's total,
144.1\,s of generation was spread over up to four concurrent lanes to give 77.56\,s of wall
clock, and only 17.3\,s went on waiting for a worker to claim a queued call, which I
obtained by differencing each
\texttt{broker.enqueue}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.claim}\,$\to$\,\texttt{broker.complete}
triple in the log. This cell is therefore not distorted by pool starvation, unlike its
chain sibling, where the same decomposition finds 1{,}100\,s of queue wait. The log's three

job ended; the dashboard's rank health table shows all eight ranks red and suspected

\section{Interpretation}

Everything structural is by construction and both checks pass: seven messages against a
closed form of $p-1=7$, three rounds against $\lceil\log_2 8\rceil=3$, so
\texttt{model\_checks\_agree} is 1 of 1 and Figure~\ref{fig:collectives} shows no red
diamond. What the \texttt{sat-} prefix promises has to be read out of the config, and the
comparison is with \texttt{real-mb-fidelity}: same $p=8$, same executor, same three
algorithms, but \texttt{facts} raised from 4 to 12 and \texttt{merge\_budget} lowered from
900 to 450, plus \texttt{kary} added at \texttt{fanin: 4}. The root must therefore render
96 items into half the space the baseline had for 32 --- a sixfold increase in pressure ---
and the baseline's own accumulators cost roughly 19.4 tokens per item, which would put the
450-token capacity at about 23 items. On that arithmetic 96 items should overflow it four
times over.

It did not saturate, and establishing why is this campaign's actual result. The
first half of the answer is re-encoding. Counting bytes per identifier in every accumulator
this run produced: 57.7, 27.4, 35.2 and 20.4 at depth 1, then 25.3 and 16.4 at depth 2,
then 14.3 at the root. The final result is not prose but a packed table,
\texttt{[F-0-0]100n [F-0-1]101d [F-0-2]102s \dots}, with the workload letters legended in
the title --- exactly the behaviour \texttt{make\_fact\_report}'s docstring describes, and
the reason the \texttt{incompressible} variant exists. The second half is that the budget
was advisory. Every one of the seven \texttt{agent\_calls} rows records \texttt{contract} =
\texttt{\{"name": "MergedReport", \dots, "max\_tokens": null, \dots, "semantics": ""\}}
while \texttt{meta} records \texttt{\{"max\_tokens": 450\}}. The number reached the
executor as a generation hint a worker may ignore, and never reached the contract checker
that would have rejected an overrun and re-issued the call. The root's 698 tokens are 55\%
over the stated limit; 96 items at 14.3 bytes is about 7.3 tokens each, or 700 tokens, so
fitting 96 items inside 450 would have required 4.7 tokens each and the operator was never
asked to try. All seven calls show \texttt{attempt: 1} and the run reports zero contract
violations, which under a null bound is uninformative rather than reassuring. The
corrected experiment is \texttt{inc-mb-fidelity-inc}: \texttt{max\_tokens: 450} on the
contract, a non-empty semantics clause, high-entropy payloads that cannot be paraphrased
shorter, and retention falls to 0.385 with four of eight ranks erased entirely.

The measurement that generalises beyond this campaign is the variance across the four
depth-1 merges. They are as close to replicates as this experiment contains --- identical
algorithm, identical fold depth, identical 933-token prompt structure, 24 items in and 24
out --- and they returned 211, 269, 356 and 491 tokens, 20.4 to 57.7 bytes per item. So
``how many tokens an item costs'' is not a property of the item in the compressible regime;
it is a choice the operator makes afresh each call, varying by a factor of 2.8. That
matters for two claims. It means a-priori saturation estimates cannot work here, which is
why \texttt{make\_tables.py} classifies capacity-boundedness empirically --- ``did some
algorithm actually lose an item'' --- rather than from a token budget. And it means the
clean 11.81-tokens-per-item arithmetic that predicts the \texttt{inc-mb} campaign's 37
survivors to within one item is a property of \emph{incompressible} content specifically:
when there is no wording freedom left, the cost per item becomes a constant and the budget
becomes a divisor.

Relative to its siblings this cell is the cost baseline, and with quality pinned at 1.000
the comparison is clean. All four algorithms do the same collective over the same 96 items
and send the same seven messages. This one uses seven operator applications over three
levels and finishes in 77.56\,s at \$0.0625; the $k$-ary tree uses three applications over
two levels and finishes in 53.07\,s at \$0.0369; the flat fold puts all seven applications
on the root and takes 197.62\,s; the chain takes 1{,}376.98\,s, of which 1{,}100\,s is a
worker failing to appear. That last figure is the one to be careful with, because
\texttt{make\_tables.py} fills \texttt{\textbackslash NFidTree},
\texttt{\textbackslash NFidChain}, \texttt{\textbackslash NFidFlat} and
\texttt{\textbackslash NFidSpeedup} from whichever broker-executed block scored 1.000 last,
which is this campaign; the published ``17.8$\times$ latency reduction for free'' is this
run's 78\,s against a chain figure that is four-fifths queueing. The structural speedup
this cell supports is $7/3$ applications on the critical path, and the honest
tree-versus-chain number from the \texttt{real-mb} baseline is 130.57 / 58.25 = 2.2$\times$.

Of the flagged findings, the 23.2\% parallel efficiency and the 3.54$\times$ imbalance are
notes and are structural for a binomial tree over eight ranks. The 17 reattachments are the
broker's normal mode. The model check is the check passing. Nothing in the list is a
defect; the defect in this campaign is the unenforced budget, and no finding can see it
because a null bound generates no violations.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is real --- \texttt{executors} records \texttt{broker}~$\times$~8 and
\texttt{worker}~$\times$~17 --- so these are agent behaviours, unlike the
\texttt{fid-synth} and \texttt{fid-inc-smoke} cells whose \texttt{function} executor
implements a drop model by construction. The most serious threat is that retention 1.000
here may not measure information transport at all. \texttt{make\_fact\_report} builds item
$i$ of rank $r$ with throughput $100 + 7r + i$ under workload
\texttt{['nominal','degraded','saturated'][i \% 3]}, so the payload is a deterministic
function of the identifier, and the packed encoding the operator converged on reproduces
that rule literally: rank 0's items are \texttt{100n 101d 102s \dots} and rank 1's are
\texttt{107n 108d \dots}. A root that had lost a whole message could continue the
arithmetic and emit the missing identifiers with correct values, and
\texttt{fact\_retention} --- which scores only whether the identifier appears --- would
call it perfect. Every retention claim in this campaign is a claim about identifier
survival on a guessable payload. The \texttt{-inc} campaign scores the six-digit serial and
four-letter checksum as well, and reports \texttt{n\_label\_only: 0}; this run reports no
such quantity because the compressible bench does not compute it.

Second, three of this run's seven results are byte-identical to results in sibling cells:
\texttt{56406e77f286} (505 tokens, ranks 4--7) is also the chain's \texttt{merge:d3} and the
$k$-ary cell's \texttt{merge:k4:d1}; \texttt{725368554341} (373 tokens, ranks 0--3) is also
the flat cell's \texttt{merge:d3} and the $k$-ary cell's other \texttt{merge:k4:d1}; and
\texttt{e411144037d6} (698 tokens, all 96 items) is also the flat cell's \texttt{merge:d7}
and the $k$-ary cell's \texttt{merge:k2:d2}. The four cells ran sequentially against one
persistent pool of Cursor subagents, so reuse is mechanically possible. I ran the
determinism test the contract requires, and it comes out against determinism: prompt digest
\texttt{20cdeba13af0} is shared between this run's \texttt{merge:d1} and the chain's and
returns 491 against 490 tokens with different digests, and \texttt{edf28c1217be} is shared
between this run's \texttt{merge:d1} and the flat cell's and returns 211 against 354.
Identical input does not give identical output here, so determinism cannot explain the
identities. What can is convergence on the canonical packed encoding: once the notation and
the arithmetic rule are fixed, one string renders a given rank set, and consistently with
that the identities occur only among the dense late accumulators and never among the verbose
early ones. Cross-run reuse cannot be excluded from the trace.

Third, one trial per algorithm. The comparison against the $k$-ary cell is safe --- 7
applications against 3, 77.56\,s against 53.07\,s, ratio 1.46 where the critical-path count
predicts 1.5, per-call latencies within 8\% of each other. The comparisons against the flat
cell and the chain are less safe: flat is credible because its mechanism is measured (seven
applications on one rank, 161.6\,s of busy time on rank 0 alone), the chain is not, for the
queueing reason above.

Finally, several accounting figures mean less than they appear to. Every \texttt{msg.recv}
records \texttt{admitted: false}, because the collective's internal receives pass
\texttt{admit=False}, so context occupancy is 0\% and high water 0 even on a rank that read
6{,}318 prompt tokens; this run exercises no admission pressure and says nothing about it.
The \$0.0625 is modelled from \$3/Mtok in and \$15/Mtok out with \texttt{tokenizer\_exact:
false}, not billed. And \texttt{rank\_position\_correlation: 0.000} is not a measurement of
positional fairness but an undefined quantity: at retention 1.000 the per-rank variance is
zero, the correlation's denominator vanishes, and the code returns 0.0 by a guard.
