\begin{abstract}
This is the deepest cell of the surrogate series at $p=16$: a fifteen-deep left fold whose
executor is \texttt{function}~$\times$~16, so it measures the protocol and the surrogate
kernel's arithmetic, not agent behaviour. Structurally it is exact ---
\texttt{fold\_depth: 15} $= p-1$, 15 rounds, 15 messages, model check passing --- and its
value to the campaign is that it makes the surrogate's actual failure mode visible in a way
the shallower cells cannot. Retention is 0.6875, and the 20 lost facts were not shed
gradually: they went in two events. The stored accumulators show that at
\texttt{merge:d4} on rank 11 the operator discarded the entire accumulated tail of ranks
12 through 15 --- sixteen items in one application --- and the output collapsed from 862
tokens to 180; one application later rank 10 discarded its own report. Everything after that
survived, so the reported \texttt{rank\_position\_correlation} of $-0.7751$ describes a
single contiguous cut near the start of the chain rather than a gradient of positional
unfairness. That is the surrogate's true granularity: because \texttt{json.dumps} emits no
newlines and the kernel splits its prompt by lines, the items it draws over are whole
inputs, so one unlucky draw erases however many ranks that input stood for. The chain is
where this is most consequential, since the accumulator arriving at depth $d$ stands for
$d$ ranks. It is also where the second pathology is largest: the kernel nests its inputs
verbatim, so output grows from 329 tokens at depth 1 to 117{,}256 at the root, the fabric
moves a 77\,kB accumulator on the final edge, and the run bills 241{,}979 output tokens for
a fold over 64 four-token facts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} is a diagonal: rank 15 acts first at $t=0.018$\,s, rank 14 at
0.019, rank 13 at 0.025, and so on down to rank 0 at 0.631\,s, one bar per lane and never
two lanes at once. That is the chain reduction with nothing else in it --- $p-1$ rounds,
each round one message and one application, the critical path equal to the whole run --- and
the peak-ranks-busy figure of 1 of 16 with a serial fraction of 100\% is the algorithm's
definition rather than a defect. The 8.00$\times$ imbalance is likewise arithmetic, and in
any case the generated findings note that occupancy is not measurable for a
\texttt{function} executor at all, since it emits no broker claim events; achieved
parallelism of 0.11$\times$ and the 99.4\% idle fraction are instrumentation artefacts.

The interesting structure is not spatial but in the widths, which accelerate. Successive
per-call latencies are 0.000\,s for the first eight merges and then 0.001, 0.001, 0.001,
0.002, 0.004, 0.008, 0.016 and 0.033\,s, so the last application alone is a third of the
run. The cause is in the outputs: 329, 532, 862, \textbf{180}, 235, 481, 840, 1{,}426,
2{,}464, 4{,}388, 8{,}140, 15{,}512, 30{,}123, 59{,}211, 117{,}256 tokens. From depth 5
onwards each application very nearly doubles its predecessor, which is what a kernel that
copies both inputs into its output does when one input is the previous accumulator. The
fabric pays for it too: the communication matrix, Figure~\ref{fig:comm}, is a clean
diagonal band whose weights grow $153, 189, 250, 496, 855, 1{,}441, 2{,}479, 4{,}403,
8{,}155, 15{,}527, 30{,}137, 59{,}226$ tokens up the chain, 125{,}073 in total, against the
binomial tree's 5{,}416 and the flat fold's 2{,}133 at the same $p$. This is the only cell
in my set where the reduction's own message traffic is the dominant cost, and it is an
artefact of the surrogate rather than of the algorithm.

The one discontinuity in that sequence is the whole story of the run's retention. The fourth
merge, on rank 11, took an 1{,}108-token prompt and returned 180 tokens. Nothing else in
the run shrinks.

\section{Interpretation}

The structure is by construction. \texttt{fold\_depth: 15} equals $p-1$, matching the
\texttt{chain} branch of \texttt{algorithms.py}, and this cell is one of the two depth-15
points in the $p=16$ series --- the other being \texttt{flat}, which is not a one-shot
$p$-way fold but $p-1$ sequential binary applications at the root, and therefore has the
same measured depth with \texttt{rounds: 1} instead of 15. The campaign's four algorithms
thus span three depths, 2, 4 and 15, not four. Fifteen messages against a predicted fifteen
and fifteen rounds against a predicted fifteen are the model check passing.

What emerged is the granularity of the surrogate's loss, and the chain exhibits it more
sharply than any other cell because the quantity at risk grows with depth. Recovering each
message payload from \texttt{runs/fid-synth/fidelity-chain-f-k4/blobs} and extracting the
identifiers gives the fold's history directly. The accumulator leaving rank 12 at depth 3
carries ranks 12, 13, 14, 15 --- sixteen real identifiers plus one phantom. The accumulator
leaving rank 11 at depth 4 carries rank 11 and the phantom, five identifiers, 189 tokens.
One application, four ranks erased. The next accumulator, leaving rank 10, still carries
only rank 11 and the phantom, so rank 10 lost its own report as well; and from depth 6 to
the root nothing further is lost, the identifier count climbing 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29, 32,
36, 40 as each remaining rank is absorbed intact. Five ranks erased, twenty facts, and
$64-20 = 44$ is the reported \texttt{n\_retained}.

The mechanism is the same one the binomial cell exposes, and it is a defect in the surrogate
rather than a property of reduction. \texttt{\_surrogate\_merge} builds its candidate list
by iterating \texttt{prompt.splitlines()} and keeping lines matching \texttt{FACT\_ID}, but
each input is interpolated as \texttt{json.dumps(\dots)} and contains no newlines, so a
binary merge prompt offers exactly three candidates: one line of the prompt's own
instructions --- \texttt{"such as [F-3-2]. Keep every identifier \dots"} --- and the two
inputs entire. The stored accumulators confirm this directly, with \texttt{findings} of
length 2 or 3 throughout and the instruction sentence always first. So the drop the
docstring describes as per-item is per-input, and the loss it describes as
$(1-r)^{\text{depth}}$ is instead a lottery in which the stake at depth $d$ is $d$ ranks.
The phantom \texttt{[F-3-2]} rides through every accumulator in the run and is scored as a
retained item; here it happens to be redundant, because rank 3's real report survived and
its per-rank retention is 1.000 either way, but in the binomial sibling the same phantom is
the sole reason rank 3 reads 0.25.

Read against the campaign, then, this cell contributes structure and a caution, not a
retention datum. Its 0.6875 ties the $k$-ary cell's and sits below the flat fold's 0.875
despite sharing the flat fold's depth, which is enough on its own to show that the numbers
in this series are not tracking depth. The reason flat did better is not shape but draws:
its fifteen prompts all have distinct lengths so its fifteen draws are effectively
independent and only two came up short, whereas here one early draw took four ranks at
once. Neither observation is evidence about operators. The evidence on the depth hypothesis
is in the broker campaigns, where the four algorithms agree exactly with one another --- at
1.000 under an advisory budget and at 0.3854 under an enforced one --- and where the
governing variable is demonstrably the enforced output budget. This run is in the advisory
regime itself: at the provenance commit \texttt{c5e8e9e} the harness passed the module-level
\texttt{MERGE\_CONTRACT}, which has no \texttt{max\_tokens} field, so the 900-token budget
was a sentence in the prompt and nothing more. That is why a 117{,}256-token output records
\texttt{attempt: 1} with \texttt{contract\_violations: 0}. A contract check that cannot fail
is not a check, and the flagged zero should be read that way.

The remaining flagged finding is the calibration warning, and it is expected here for a
reason worth naming: the fit was rejected because it produced a negative intercept
($\alpha = -0.0001$\,s) on $R^2 = 0.9987$. The relationship really is almost perfectly
linear in output size --- the surrogate's cost is string concatenation --- but the intercept
is smaller than the timer's resolution, so the guard fires. That is the guard behaving
correctly on a run whose latencies are microseconds, not a defect, and it means the
published $\alpha = 0.003$\,s and $\beta = 10^{-6}$\,s/token are medians rather than a
model.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The executor is a surrogate, so no claim here transfers to agents. In particular the
runaway token growth, the 125{,}073 tokens of message traffic and the modelled \$4.0162 are
properties of a kernel that concatenates rather than summarises; a real operator asked to
merge two reports into at most 900 tokens would not return 117{,}256, and the broker chain
cells at $p=8$ return 387--695. Anyone comparing wall times or costs across my fifteen runs
must partition them by executor first.

The fold reconstruction is a measurement, but an indirect one. The \texttt{function}
executor writes no rows to \texttt{agent\_calls}, so this run's fabric holds no prompts and
no results; what I read were the fifteen message payloads under \texttt{blobs/}, which are
the accumulators as sent, and I inferred each application's effect by differencing
consecutive payloads along the chain. That is sound for the interior of the chain but says
nothing about the root's own final application, whose result was never sent --- the claim
that ranks 0 through 9 all survived to the end rests on the results file's
\texttt{per\_rank\_retention}, not on a blob I read. Similarly, ``the drop at depth 4 took
the accumulator rather than rank 11's own report'' is read off the identifier sets, which
is unambiguous, but the reason that particular draw failed is not recoverable without
replaying the kernel, and unlike the binomial cell's five identical prompts I did not
replay it here.

Two accounting caveats. \texttt{tokens\_unadmitted: 125{,}073} equals
\texttt{tokens\_sent}, and every \texttt{msg.recv} records \texttt{admitted: false}, because
the collective's internal receives pass \texttt{admit=False}; so context occupancy reads 0\%
on a rank that received a 59{,}226-token payload, and this run says nothing about admission
pressure even though it is the one run in my set that would obviously have exhausted a real
context. That is a genuine gap. And \texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}, so all token counts are
the repository's approximation.
