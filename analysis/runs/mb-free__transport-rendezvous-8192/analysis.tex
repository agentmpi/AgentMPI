\begin{abstract}
This is the first cell of a ten-cell transport sweep at which the mode is not a
preference. Eight agent-free ranks ring-exchange an 8{,}192-word payload under
\texttt{Mode.RENDEZVOUS}: 34 events, 0.06\,s, all eight messages delivered, zero
admissions, a context high water of zero on every rank and 86{,}232 tokens deferred.
Its eager twin, given the same payload, produced 18 events and no messages at all ---
\texttt{results/microbench/free-transport.json} records \texttt{completed: false},
\texttt{n\_failed: 8}, \texttt{ERR\_CONTEXT\_OVERFLOW} --- because the payload's
10{,}779 estimated tokens exceed the destination's 4{,}096-token unexpected-message
credit by $2.63\times$ and \texttt{\_await\_eager\_credit} refuses it before writing
a fabric row. The pair is unusually clean evidence: the two runs put the
byte-identical blob \texttt{3fbdeea17da4} into their content stores, address the same
ring, and differ in nothing except what the envelope carries. Eager put the payload
in it and was refused; rendezvous put a handle in it and was not. This is the cell
that turns AgentMPI's transport-mode argument from a design preference into a
requirement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reading the timeline}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows eight lanes carrying two grey lifecycle diamonds and
two green message ticks each, and no bars: the executor is \texttt{none} on every
rank, there were zero agent calls, and no \texttt{task.*} event exists for a bar to
span. Read ``idle fraction 100.0\%'' and ``achieved parallelism 0.00$\times$'' in the
headline table as saying that no rank ever held a task. Ranks arrive over a 33\,ms
window inside a 56\,ms span, and the receive latencies have grown: median 10\,ms and
maximum 31\,ms, against 5\,ms and 7\,ms at 2{,}048 words. That is not the received
payload moving --- nothing is transferred on a rendezvous receive, and
\texttt{sent\_at} is stamped after the sender's own \texttt{fabric.blobs.put} has
already completed. It is that every rank sends before it receives, so a message's
measured latency includes the receiver's own time writing its 40\,KiB outgoing blob
before it ever looks in its mailbox. The same term triples the span against the
2{,}048-word cell's 20\,ms. Nothing blocked: there is no
\texttt{transport.credit\_stall} in the log, and none is possible, because the eager
credit mechanism does not apply to rendezvous traffic at all.
Figure~\ref{fig:comm} shows the ring as a single off-diagonal band of 10{,}779 tokens
per edge.

The comparison worth making is with a figure that does not exist. The eager cell at
this size has no \texttt{figures/comm.pdf} --- \texttt{analyze\_run.py} does not draw
a matrix for a run with no traffic --- and its timeline has only the grey lifecycle
marks, with the viewer's legend omitting the messages swatch entirely. Here the rank
health table reads 0\% occupancy across all eight ranks and the stats row reads
\texttt{TOKENS DEFERRED 86.2k}; there it reads 0\% occupancy and \texttt{0.0k}
deferred, and the zeros mean the opposite thing. This is the sweep's sharpest
illustration of why the trace must be read alongside the results file: two runs whose
dashboards both show \texttt{FAILURES 0} and all ranks \texttt{finalized}, one of
which delivered everything and one of which delivered nothing.

\section{Interpretation}

By construction: the world size, the ring mapping, one send and one receive per rank,
and the mode --- and here the forcing runs the other way from the eager column. Every
\texttt{rank.init} records \texttt{eager\_limit: 1000000000}, so
\texttt{\_choose\_mode}'s automatic threshold is off; had it been left at the shipped
\texttt{DEFAULT\_EAGER\_LIMIT} of 2{,}048 tokens, the runtime would have selected
rendezvous for this 10{,}779-token payload on its own. This cell is what AgentMPI
does by default, and its eager twin is what happens when a harness overrides that
default at a size where the override cannot work. That framing matters for reading
the sweep: the failure in the eager column is not AgentMPI failing, it is AgentMPI
refusing an instruction it was given, and refusing it early, locally and with the
remedy named in the exception message.

The mechanism of the contrast is worth stating precisely, because the two runs are so
nearly identical that it is easy to assume rendezvous saved something physical. It
did not. Both runs' \texttt{send} calls reached
\texttt{fabric.blobs.put(payload)} and wrote all 40{,}960 bytes; the blob is present
on disk in \emph{both} run directories, under the same digest, because the content is
the same and the store is content-addressed. Both would have sent eight envelopes.
The divergence happens at the next line: \texttt{\_choose\_mode} returns
\texttt{Mode.EAGER} for the twin, which enters \texttt{\_await\_eager\_credit}, which
compares 10{,}779 against the destination's \texttt{unexpected\_limit} of 4{,}096 and
raises \texttt{AmpiContextOverflow} on the spot --- before the \texttt{messages} row,
before the sequence-number increment, which is why that run's fabric has empty
\texttt{messages} and \texttt{seqs} tables. Here \texttt{\_choose\_mode} returns
\texttt{Mode.RENDEZVOUS}, the credit check is skipped entirely, and the envelope goes
out carrying digest, token count, kind and a one-line synopsis. The difference between
a run that works and a run that does not is one branch on a mode flag. Note also
which limit did the refusing: 10{,}779 tokens would have fitted inside the receiver's
32{,}768-token context budget, at 33\% occupancy. The eager path did not fail because
the message was too big for the receiver; it failed because it was too big for the
receiver's unexpected-message pool, a flow-control bound set at $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of the
context budget in this benchmark.

The deferral figure needs one correction. \texttt{metrics.json} reports 86{,}232,
which is $8 \times 10{,}779$ --- one rendezvous send per rank, exactly equal to
\texttt{tokens sent} because all the traffic is rendezvous, and consistent with the
128-, 512- and 2{,}048-word cells at $4.00\times$ the last of them for $4\times$ the
payload. The results file records 172{,}464, and the per-rank events show
\texttt{tokens\_deferred: 21558} against \texttt{tokens\_sent: 10779}: twice what the
rank transmitted. Before commit \texttt{0fea51d2},
\texttt{RankCost.tokens\_deferred} accumulated on rendezvous sends \emph{and} on every
non-admitted receive, so each rank counted its own send and its predecessor's
arrival. The commit split the field into \texttt{tokens\_deferred} (send-side,
rendezvous only) and \texttt{tokens\_unadmitted} (receive-side, any mode), under which
this run reports 86{,}232 of each. \texttt{cost.summarise} derives from the log and
was always right, which is why the viewer and \texttt{metrics.json} already show the
corrected number. The fix's summary holds that the paper's transport table is
unaffected because the microbench admits its receives; that is true of the eager
rows, which correctly read zero, and false of the rendezvous rows, since
\texttt{bench\_transport} never passes \texttt{admit} and rendezvous defaults it to
\texttt{False}. Every rendezvous entry in that table is exactly doubled.

\section{Threats to this reading}

The strongest caveat is that this cell's advantage is entirely deferral, and deferral
is not delivery. No \texttt{fetch} is called anywhere in the transport sweep, so the
eight receivers finished holding a digest, a token count and a synopsis of a document
they never read. If the workload genuinely needed the content, a rank would have to
call \texttt{fetch} with \texttt{admit=True} and would then charge 10{,}779 tokens to
a 32{,}768-token budget --- affordable once, not four times. The cell therefore shows
that a large artefact can be \emph{addressed} without cost, not that it can be
\emph{used} without cost, and the mechanism that would bridge the two, a partial
materialisation through a \texttt{View}, is exercised nowhere in this sweep. Second,
the failure of the eager twin --- the comparison this document rests on --- is not
recorded in that run's trace. Its \texttt{job.finish} says \texttt{ok: true} with
\texttt{failed\_ranks: []}, its \texttt{metrics.json} says \texttt{degraded: false},
and the error class comes from the results file; the trace corroborates only by
absence, through 18 events instead of 34 and empty fabric tables. The inference that
the immediate precondition fired, rather than the credit loop timing out, rests on
that run's 0.04\,s span against a 12\,s stall timeout and on the absence of
\texttt{transport.credit\_stall}. Third, token counts are estimates
(\texttt{tokenizer\_exact: false}): 10{,}779 is $\lceil 40960/3.8 \rfloor$ from a
character heuristic on one repeated word, and a real tokeniser would price it near
8{,}192 --- still $2.0\times$ the credit, so the contrast survives, but every
deferred figure here scales by about $0.76$. Fourth, both budgets are benchmark
parameters rather than defaults (\texttt{unexpected\_limit} 4{,}096 against a shipped
$8\times$ eager limit; \texttt{context\_budget} 32{,}768 against 128{,}000), so this
pair locates a ceiling the benchmark chose. Finally, the executor is \texttt{none}
with zero agent calls and the wall time is one sample dominated by a 40\,KiB blob
write and eight SQLite-backed rank start-ups, so nothing here is a statement about
agents or about throughput.
