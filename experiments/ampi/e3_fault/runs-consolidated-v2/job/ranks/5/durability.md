Durable shared state separates ownership of work from the lifetime of the participant that began it. When an agent publishes its draft, progress marker, inputs, and relevant metadata to storage that survives the agent, those artifacts remain available after a crash or lease expiry. A survivor can inspect the durable record, distinguish completed output from genuinely missing work, and atomically claim only the unfinished portion. This prevents both silent loss and wasteful reconstruction: already published material is reused, while claim records keep multiple survivors from performing the same recovery task. Durability also gives recovery a stable checkpoint rather than forcing it to infer intent from transient messages or process memory. The replacement participant may have a different rank or execution context, but it can resume from the shared protocol state, preserve the failed participant’s useful contribution, and complete the report without requiring that participant to return.
