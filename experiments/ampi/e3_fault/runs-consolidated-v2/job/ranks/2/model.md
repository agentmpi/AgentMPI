The failure model treats each participant as an independent executor whose behavior can degrade in three distinct ways. A crash failure stops execution abruptly, so no further messages, heartbeats, or cleanup appear, although data committed to durable shared storage remains available. A stall leaves the executor alive but unable to make timely progress; peers detect it through an expired lease or missing heartbeat and must avoid waiting forever on collective operations. The hardest case is a semantic failure: the participant remains responsive and produces fluent, confident output that is nevertheless incorrect, inconsistent with evidence, or incompatible with the protocol. Such answers cannot be identified by liveness checks alone. Recovery therefore combines failure detection and communicator repair for crashes or stalls with validation, provenance, redundancy, and agreement for semantic errors. The model assumes asynchronous observation, so delayed work can resemble failure until a declared heartbeat deadline passes.
